# Changelog

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.0.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## Unreleased

### Fixed

- `anri.index.mask.backproject` (and so `reconstruct` and `anri.index.diagnostics.back_projection`) failed with float64 enabled (`jax_enable_x64`): the beam direction came out float64 and its rows' weights no longer matched the float32 accumulator. Each row's contribution is now cast to the accumulator's dtype.
- Padded voxels no longer take intensity in the voxel fit. `anri.index.occupancy.pad_voxels` padded with voxels at (1e6, 1e6), which sit on the rotation axis's ray at omega = 135 and 315 degrees and so took part in candidate selection and MLEM there (without absorption). Padding is now marked by z = NaN and `system` gives it no entries. Reported in [#51](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/51).
- On a full-turn scan, a prediction within half an omega bin of the seam keeps the corner on the other side (`anri.index.data.eta_omega_corners` wraps omega when n_o b_o = 360, as it wraps eta). It lost up to half its weight there, in the filter and the voxel fit. Partial scans still drop the corners past their ends. Reported in [#51](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/51).
- Fly and helical scans: each (row, omega bin)'s dty offset and exposure (`anri.index.data.dty_offsets`) take the same frames as the histogram. Frames past the scan's ends were wrapped into the other end, and the histogram stage put frames in rows by each row's mean dty instead of the DataSet's dty that binned them. Reported in [#51](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/51).
- `anri.index.occupancy.censored_ratio` and `deviance` are jitted, so XLA fuses them instead of making several histogram-sized temporaries per MLEM iteration. Reported in [#51](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/51).

### Changed

- `anri.fwd.render.render_row` has one outline for both ways of rendering (select the peaks, give each a window size, render one size at a time, merge): the per-voxel windows (`_render_windows`) and splat (`_render_spots`) differ only in how a batch is rendered. Pixels and stats are unchanged (bit for bit, with and without splat, several window sizes and small batches).
- `simulate_sparse` and `write_rendered_rows` moved from `anri.io.imaged11` to `anri.simulate.stages`: the loop that renders a scan's rows belongs with the render stage, and `anri.io` no longer imports the renderer. `anri.io.imaged11.write_scan` still writes each row.
- `anri.index.occupancy.add_cells` and `take_cells`: the masked scatter-add and gather into histogram cells that both MLEMs (the voxel fit and the filter's orientation fit) each wrote out four times.
- One function turns a stage's recorded options back into its command line: `anri.io.provenance.command_line`, which reads the subcommand's argparse options (separate values, a repeated flag, comma-separated, or `--no-x` for an option on by default). `run_command` in `anri.index.stages`, `anri.calib.stages` and `anri.simulate.stages` each had its own copy of these rules. Each command line is built by a `parser()` function in its `__main__`, and `--iter` (and `anri.calib rings --mask`) now parse to the stage's own option names, `n_iter` (and `detector_mask`).
- The indexer's selection helpers and constants moved out of `anri.index.stages`, next to what they select, so that plots and diagnostics no longer import the stages: `select_by_likelihood` to `anri.index.orientations`; `select_occupied`, `select_populations`, `occupancy_weighted` and `N_POP` to `anri.index.populations`; `lit_map` to `anri.index.search`; `voxel_grid` to `anri.index.mask`; `sample_groups` and the histogram bins `B_E`, `B_O`, `R_E`, `R_O` to `anri.index.data`.
- An ImageD11 phase's cell keys (`cell__a` ... `cell_lattice_[P,A,B,C,I,F,R]`) are read and written in one place: `anri.io.imaged11.lattice_from_cell` and `cell_from_lattice`, used by the indexer, the calibration and the simulation (each had its own copy of the keys). The calibration's `rings` stage now says a centring letter needs a space-group number, as the indexer did.
- Figures that survive a restart of the Jupyter server: the Guides page says how (JupyterLab's *Save Widget State Automatically*, `"saveState": true`). ipympl then saves each figure as last drawn in the notebook, and after a restart it shows as a picture where "Error displaying widget: model not found" was; checked in JupyterLab 4.6 with ipympl 0.10 (it fixes ImageD11's notebooks too). Every guide notebook with widgets also has a static mode for the docs: `interactive = False` (now also in the calibration and indexing-parameters guides, as in the rendering and frame-comparison guides) draws each figure once at the parameters' values as an inline image, with no widgets. Drawing a mask by hand still needs `interactive = True`.
- The calibration guide takes every option of `python -m anri.calib rings`: a dense powder image instead of the scan's own pixels (`image`), and `ring_phase`, `detector_mask`, `spatial`, `scans`, `threshold`, `ring_iter`, `n_eta`, `ring_clip` and `merge`. Its last cell prints every command that remakes the calibration from the dataset (`anri.calib.stages.commands`).
- A theory page on calibrating the geometry (`docs/source/theory/calibration.rst`): the powder rings (each (ring, η sector)'s residual, the Levenberg-Marquardt fit in rounds, why tilt_x and the wavelength are left out, the centre and distance searches), the scan's peaks (matching along the beam with parallax, the residuals, the Schur complement over the orientations, what the scan separates that rings cannot, the rounds), and the strain left. The theory section now covers `anri.calib`.
- A calibration's provenance goes back to its dataset through the powder rings. `anri.io.imaged11.write_pars(..., source=...)` records in `pars.json` the stage file it was written from, with its id (`"anri_source"`; ImageD11 reads only `geometry` and `phases`), and `python -m anri.calib pars` (`anri.calib.stages.write_pars`) gives it. The indexer's histogram stage, given such a `pars.json`, records that file as its source if it is still there and unchanged (it logs why not otherwise), so `show` prints the rings, the indexing and the calibration in one chain. `anri.calib.stages.commands` returns every command that remakes a calibration or rings file from the dataset (`rings`, `pars`, the indexer's `run` with `--outdir`, `fit`), and `run_command` now also takes a rings file. `anri.index.stages.run_command` stops at the latest histogram stage, so a calibration before it adds none of its options. `python -m anri.calib rings --merge` (the ring merging distance) was missing from the command line.
- `python -m anri.calib fit` converges, and refuses a local search's map. Each round re-matches the peaks with the new geometry. After the listed tolerances, the last is now repeated while a round still moves a free parameter by more than its standard deviation (`--extra-rounds`, default 6). If it still has not converged it says so: its quoted errors are then too small. A local search's file is refused, naming its fit stage file. Its orientations, 0.11 deg apart, let each round's matching pick the one that absorbs part of the geometry's error, so the rounds close only ~20% of the gap each. On the Ni phantom, from a powder-ring start, distance and wavelength were still 1e-4 off after the default rounds, against 1e-6 from the fit stage's map. The full chain on that phantom (`anri.calib rings` from a start 7% off in distance, 1.8% in wavelength and 100 px in centre, then `anri.index`, then `anri.calib fit` on the fit map) ends within 6e-6 in distance and wavelength, 1e-4 px in centre and 1e-7 rad in the tilts. It is also faster: 54 s on a shared GPU for 802k peaks and 9 rounds, against 89 s for 4 rounds before. Matching is in float32 (8x faster on a GPU than float64; matches identical but for near-ties). Peaks are padded to one size for every round, so nothing recompiles between rounds. Empty map columns are dropped (10 of 64 used). `--row-step N` uses the peaks of every Nth dty row only, for CPU-only machines: 53 s on 16 CPU cores for the Ni phantom with `--row-step 10`, same geometry as all rows within 1e-6.
- `anri.utils.setup` turns on JAX's persistent compilation cache (`cache_dir`, default `JAX_COMPILATION_CACHE_DIR` or `~/.cache/anri/jax`; "" turns it off). A later run reuses what an earlier one compiled: `python -m anri.calib fit` on the Ni phantom takes 23 s instead of 40 s on a GPU the second time (compiling 1.4 s instead of 18 s). A changed function gets a new key and compiles again. The cache is capped at 2 GB only if the `filelock` package is installed (without it JAX turns the whole cache off when given a cap). The JAX used here (0.8.2) keeps only GPU compiles.
- Every iteration of a SQUAREM-accelerated MLEM fit (the voxel fit, each local-search level, the filter's orientation fit) is now logged, with its step length, whether the extrapolated step was taken, and the deviance; it was every fifth. The numbers were on the host already, so this adds no transfers.

- The docs' user guide follows rietx's split: Tutorials, which teach Anri in order, and a new Guides section, each page one task ("How to ..."): simulating a dataset (`rendering_parameters`), choosing the indexing parameters, comparing measured frames with a forward model, and exporting a powder pattern. The tutorials' geometry prose, which repeated the Theory section, is now a pointer to the Reference frames page. Five tutorials are rewritten to go with the Theory pages and check them: crystallography (the B matrix of a hexagonal cell, the UBI's rows, an extinction by atom positions, the Laue group), the goniometer (each rotation's sign, wedge and chi tilting the omega axis, ImageD11's wedge sign, dty and y0 giving each voxel's sinusoid), the detector (pixel to lab and back, the o-matrix, tilts, and FABLE's test case), a simple forward model (every centroid of a grain with `anri.fwd.peak.get_centroid_both`, which ImageD11 indexes back to the same grain) and the forward model with errors (position, wavelength and divergence widths, each checked against its formula, the vertical divergence's |cot eta| over every reflection of a grain).

- `python -m anri.index --mask auto` is now the sample's support in the projections (`anri.index.mask.support_mask`): the voxels whose row is lit at every omega the scan covers them (each omega's projection scaled to its bright rows and median-filtered along omega), largest region, convex hull. It is no longer cut to the disc, so a sample larger than the disc is fitted whole. The previous automatic mask, a threshold of a filtered back-projection of log intensities, picked out the bright edges of a strongly absorbing sample and lost its inside. On a large, coarse-grained Ag2S sample the support still rounds the corners (a row clipping a corner crosses little material, so few spots), so `disc` (with `--pad`) stays the default. The fit stage saves the support image as `support` (was `recon`).
- The theory pages follow the code again. Intensities: the renderer models absorption along each path, from the map itself or a given map of coefficients (it was described as a transmission per frame only). A new page, "Sharpening orientations off the grid", covers the local search (`anri.index.local`). Populations: the grouping radius is 1.8 steps of the search grid even after a local search, and `occupied` and `min_frac` choose what is reported. The voxel grid: `grid_pad` and `gridstep`. The rendering guide gains the absorption options (`cif`, `mu`, `mu_map`, `absorption`), `pol_factor`, `batch` and `peaks_table`, and is stored with a run at this version; the frame-comparison guide gains the instrument's options (`divergence`, `bandwidth`, `beam_fwhm`, `psf`, `pol_factor`) and absorption, and shows how its model was made. The powder guide lists its options.

### Fixed

- `anri.index.diagnostics.back_projection` reconstructs by default on the histogram stage's grid (with `--grid-pad`), as the fit and the disc mask do; it used the unpadded grid, so in the indexing guide's mask section (section 3) a padded grid put the sample off-centre and the threshold slider failed (shapes 417 and 469).

### Removed

- The forward model's `_both` twins and pre-built vmaps: `anri.fwd.peak.hkl_to_k_omega_both` and `get_centroid_both`, `anri.fwd.box.get_centroid_box_both`, `get_centroid_box_all(_grains)(_both)` and `propagate_cov_box_all(_grains)`, `anri.fwd.scan.get_centroid_scan_both`, `get_centroid_scan_all(_grains)(_both)` and `propagate_cov_scan_all(_grains)(_both)`. Each centroid is now one function of one peak: vmap over `etasign` (`jnp.array([1.0, -1.0])`) for both peaks of a reflection, and over voxels or hkls as needed (the tutorials and examples show how). The renderer and the calibration do so; their outputs are unchanged (bit for bit, on a 27k-voxel box-beam row and the calibration's predictions), and render time too. The calibration's per-peak residual now computes only the peak's own branch.
- `anri.refine` (and its tutorial), which was never released: refining a map against the pixels will be redone from a design that represents each population's orientation spread.

### Added

- Grain maps for the renderer, e.g. from DCT: one UBI per grain and a grain label per voxel, so no orientation spread and one strain per grain. `anri.fwd.render.grain_entries` makes map entries from a label volume ([nx, ny, nz] cubes or [nx, ny] columns, centred on the rotation axis, sample axes) and the grains' UBIs: "grain" [N] and "grain_ubi" [G, 3, 3] in place of "ubi". `render_row` renders them as the same map with every voxel given its grain's UBI (the same peaks and pixels, tested), but finds the peaks that reach a row grain by grain first: a peak's omega does not depend on where its voxel is, so each (grain, hkl, branch) is tested against the row's omega range once, and only the voxels of those that pass are placed on the detector. On a DCT-like map (216k voxels, 10 grains, 78 hkls, 10 deg of frames, 4 CPU cores) selection takes 0.19 s instead of 2.5 s and the whole row 10.4 s instead of 13.4 s; the rest is integrating each peak over its window cells, which is per voxel either way. `render_row(..., splat=True)` renders a grain map spot by spot, a spot being a (grain, hkl, branch): every voxel's peak in it has the same omega and, to ~ voxel offset / detector distance, the same covariance, so each voxel's weight (density, beam, absorption, per frame) is shared bilinearly between the four pixels around its own (sc, fc) on a tile, and each frame of the tile is convolved once with one peak's window cells (`jax.lax.conv_general_dilated`, depthwise). Against the per-voxel render (tested): totals within 0.15%, pixels within 2% of the brightest, frame centroids within 0.01 px; on the map above, 1.4 s instead of 15.8 s for the row (11x), and 0.26 s instead of 6.0 s (23x) for 1 um voxels on 1 um pixels. Each device takes whole spots with their voxels, so nothing is merged across devices before the host. `sig_rot` must then be the same over each grain. The per-voxel cut `min_value` applies to the summed spot, not to each voxel's share, so faint voxels add up instead of being dropped one by one. `python -m anri.simulate prepare` reads a grain map from an `.npz` (with "grain" and "grain_ubi" instead of "ubi") and keeps its grains through render. Per-voxel maps, and everything that reads them (indexer, calibration), are unchanged.
- `python -m anri.simulate` simulates box-beam scans (DCT, 3DXRD) as well as scanning 3DXRD. `prepare --beam box` lights the whole sample with a flat-topped beam (`--beam-size H V`, default the sample plus two voxels; `--beam-fwhm` is then its edges' blur, default a voxel) in one rotation at y0, with cubes for a map of several layers; `--beam pencil` (the default) is unchanged. `render --splat` renders grain maps spot by spot, and `--frames-per-call N` renders each row N frames at a time (`anri.io.imaged11.write_rendered_rows`), so that a long rotation fits in memory; each call renders only its own frames, so the parts join without overlap. The DCT tutorial builds its map with `grain_entries` and renders with `splat`: 136 s for its 360° scan (512k voxels, 28M voxel peaks in 837 spots) on 4 CPU cores, against 784 s voxel by voxel; a new section renders 10° both ways (5 s against 60 s): totals within 0.15%, pixels within 1% of the brightest. Splat's kernels are computed once per window class, and voxels are added onto the tiles in fixed chunks, so a scan compiles a few times, not once per batch.
- Extinction, per phase, in the forward model (Becker and Coppens, 1974, after Zachariasen, 1967): `anri.diffract.factors.extinction_parameters` gives each reflection's primary and secondary extinction parameters from its absolute `|F|^2`, the cell volume, the wavelength and the phase's mosaic crystal (radius of a perfect domain, radius of the crystal, width of a Gaussian or Lorentzian mosaic distribution), with the absorption-weighted mean path through the crystal when an attenuation coefficient is given (first order, good to `mu R` about 1). `extinction` is their analytic fit (within 4% of their tables for sin(theta) <= 0.5), and `polarisation_extinction` extinguishes the two field components separately, as they do. `render_row` applies it when a phase's entries carry `"extinction": (x, X, gaussian)` (per reflection); without it nothing changes. Strong reflections of perfect crystals, such as a calibrant Si cube, come out weaker than kinematic `|F|^2`: on an undeformed Si cube at 65 keV the strong (h + k + l = 4n) reflections at low angle were 10-45% below their weak neighbours. The theory page on intensities has a section on it.
- The indexing guide (`docs/source/guides/indexing_parameters.ipynb`) uses the indexer's new options. Section 1 picks any rings from a list, not only the first (`rings`, `ring_merge`): the table lists every ring shown, used or not, with its multiplicity, `|F|^2` and gap. `anri.index.diagnostics.ring_windows` and `anri.index.plots.plot_rings` take a ring list as the histogram stage does, and measure each ring's width between its true neighbours. `ring_profile_cake` takes `merge`. Section 2 passes `detector_mask`, `dty_source` and `frame_dty`, and draws the detector coverage of each ring (`plot_coverage`). Section 9 sets `levels`, `cover` and `per_voxel` in the parameters, and has plots before and after the local search. Before: how many orientations each voxel needs to hold `cover` of its occupancy (`orientations_needed`, `plot_local_slots`, using `anri.index.local.covering`, now public), and the children a level predicts. After: the deviance at each level (`level_recorder`), and how far each voxel's main population moved, with its fraction and spread before and after (`local_change`, `plot_local`). The populations section draws the occupancy-weighted IPF colours (`ipf_z_occupancy`), and the last section points to each stage file's log.
- `anri.calib.diagnostics` and `anri.calib.plots` hold the scan fit's diagnostics, which were inline in the calibration guide, so that a workflow widget can bind them as the notebook does: `read_occupancy` and `occupancy_kept` with `plot_min_occ` (`--min-occ`; the stage's own cut, `anri.calib.stages.select_occupancy`), `match_distances` with `plot_match` (`--tols`, and again at the calibrated geometry), and for the result `read_calib`, `residual_trends` (`plot_residuals`, `plot_residual_hists`) and `strain_left` (`plot_strain_left`). `anri.calib.fit.split_strain` splits the strain left into hydrostatic and deviatoric parts and their peak-weighted means, for both the stage's log and the plot. The guide calls them, with the same numbers as before.
- The calibration guide (`docs/source/guides/calibration.ipynb`) runs the whole chain from a first guess far off. It runs `python -m anri.calib rings` on the scan's own sparse pixels, with plots of the centre search, the distance search and each ring's offset per η sector before and after the fit (`anri.calib.plots`, from `anri.calib.diagnostics.read_rings`). It then indexes with the rings' geometry (fit stage, no local search) and runs `python -m anri.calib fit`. `rings.find_centre` and `find_distance` also return their curves, which `stage_rings` stores in the file's `search` group. The phantom is now the 316L phantom's grains without their cells and twins: from the distance 7% short, the wavelength 1.8% short and the centre 60 and 90 px off, the chain ends 5e-6 from the true distance and wavelength (5 rounds), the centre within 0.01 px. The guide and `stage_fit` state the scope: calibrants and little-deformed samples. On the 316L phantom's cells (a few tenths of a degree) the rounds converge slowly, still 6e-4 out after ten, and the stage warns. A deformed sample's geometry is refined with its strains.
- The forward model models absorption along the incoming and diffracted paths, as the indexer does, with the same paths (`anri.geom.paths`). `anri.fwd.render.add_absorption` gives each map entry its optical depth to the sample's edge in every direction in the slice; `render_row` then scales each window frame by exp(-(depth in + depth out)) at that frame's omega. The sample is built from the map itself (`anri.geom.paths.attenuation_map`): a voxel holding entries is full, with its phases' attenuation coefficients weighted by their densities, so a multiphase map absorbs phase by phase; one slice per layer of a 3D map. Or it is given as a map of coefficients, e.g. from an absorption tomography (pores and unindexed phases are then included). `python -m anri.simulate prepare` models it by default when every phase has a CIF (`anri.crystal.structure.attenuation`) or `--mu` (one value, or NAME=VALUE per phase), or with `--mu-map` (a `.npy`); `--no-absorption` turns it off. `edge_distances` now also takes a map of coefficients, giving optical depths.
- `python -m anri.calib rings` (`anri.calib.stages.stage_rings`, `anri.calib.rings`, `<...>_rings.h5`): the first calibration step, as ImageD11's powder calibration. It fits the distance, beam centre, tilt_y and tilt_z, from the motors' values, to a known phase's powder rings, close enough for the sample to index. tilt_x moves no ring and the wavelength is degenerate with the distance, so both are left for `anri.calib fit`. The data are a dense powder image, or a DataSet's sparse pixels summed over every frame (`--scans` for fewer), with 2theta seen from the lab origin, as ImageD11 computes it. Each lit pixel is assigned to the nearest ring within a tolerance, re-assigned at every iteration so the window follows the ring. Each (ring, 1 deg eta sector) gives one residual, the intensity-weighted mean 2theta of its pixels minus the ring's, as each sector's peak does in ImageD11. The fit is Levenberg-Marquardt in rounds of tolerance (`--tols 0.2,0.1`; narrower windows, under a few pixels, alias). Rings closer than twice the widest tolerance to a neighbour are left out, since overlapping profiles pull each other's means. Since the motors' centre can be 100 px off, more than a ring gap, a search without ring assignment comes first (`--no-search` skips it). The centre is where the histogram of the pixels' radii is sharpest (`rings.find_centre`, grids of 8, 2 and 0.5 px up to `--max-shift` 200 px). The distance is the one whose ring windows catch the most intensity above a flat background (`rings.find_distance`, `--span 0.7 1.4` x the start). On ID11's CeO2 calibration image, from the motors (distance 240 mm, centre 38 and 102 px off), it lands where it does from a close start, 3 s in all: distance +11 um, centre 0.01 px and tilts 6e-5 from ImageD11's powder notebook result (+3 um, 0.01 px and 2e-5 with `--tols 0.1,0.05`). An undeformed Si crystal's sparse pixels (one crystal, 18k lit pixels) also work, from a centre 108 px and a distance 7% off. The file holds each sector's offset from its ring before and after (the straightened rings), and `pars` writes it as a `pars.json` to index with. On the Ni phantom's sparse pixels (207 scans, 8 s on CPU), from a distance 1% off, the centre 3 px and the tilts 2 mrad off, it returns to the truth within 2 sigma: 0.6 um (distance), 0.004 px (centre) and 2e-5 rad (tilts). With the wavelength also 1.8% off and kept, the distance absorbs it (+2%) and every ring falls within 0.014 deg of its prediction.
- An agent skill for running anri (`anri/skill/anri`, in the open Agent Skills format, shipped in the package; issue #48): `SKILL.md` gives the order of work, the questions to ask first (optics, energy, beam width, segmentation cut, monitor), how to run on a shared machine, which stage's output chooses each option, what to check in a stage file before believing it (its log, the row ratios, convergence, candidates, completeness) and what a map can claim. Reference files cover the indexer stage by stage (each option, the number that chooses it and the diagnostic or plot that computes it, and what each file holds), simulating and the forward model, calibrating the geometry (`python -m anri.calib`: rings, indexing, then the scan fit; what to ask first, the plot or number behind each option, and the residual and strain-left checks before believing a calibration), making a phantom to test a method (`anri.phantom`: which options give which microstructure, what it cannot make, the checks before using it, and how to judge a method on it), and the physics behind the rules. `tests/unit/test_skill.py` checks that every command, option, function and link it names exists, so a rename fails CI. The README says how to link it where an agent looks for skills.
- Each `anri.index`, `anri.simulate` and `anri.calib` stage file keeps its log: the dataset `provenance/log` holds every message the stage logged, timed from its start, whichever front-end ran it (command line, notebook, or a workflow system). A stage wraps the `log` it is given with `anri.io.provenance.recorder` and stores the messages with `write_log`; `read_provenance` returns them as `"log"` (empty for older files), and `show --log` (`python -m anri.index`, `python -m anri.simulate` and `python -m anri.calib`; `show(path, log=True)`) prints them for every stage of the chain.
- A calibration guide (`docs/source/guides/calibration.ipynb`, #47): runs `python -m anri.calib fit` from Jupyter, with a plot for each choice (the map's occupancy cut, the matching tolerance against the distance of each peak's (h, k, l) from integers) and for the result (residuals against omega, detector azimuth and dty; the strain left per orientation), then writes ImageD11 parameter files and prints the command line. With no map given it renders the 316L phantom with a wrong geometry, segments and indexes it. `anri.calib.stages.load_inputs` reads what the stage starts from (used by `stage_fit` too), and `anri.calib.stages.run_command` gives the command that remakes a calibration file.
- `python -m anri.index localsearch` (`anri.index.stages.stage_localsearch`, `<...>_localsearch.h5`): a stage between fit and populations that sharpens the fit's orientations off the search grid, around those each voxel uses (`anri.index.local`). Each level replaces every orientation a voxel uses (its most occupied, holding `--cover` of its occupancy, at most `--per-voxel`) by the 27 rotations of a 3 x 3 x 3 grid around it, a third of the step apart (`local.children`), and fits each voxel again over the children of its own orientations only (`local.inherit`, `fit_occupancy(given=...)`), with the fit's options. `run` includes it with `--levels 2` (a 1 deg grid to 0.11 deg; `--levels 0` skips it), and the populations stage takes either file. On the search grid alone, a twin whose grid point lands nearer the exact twin than the grain's own grid point lands to the grain explains the reflections the two share, and takes occupancy where there is no twin: on an undeformed Si cube a Sigma3 ghost held 30% of the occupancy and the crystal 15% (3 deg grid); with the local search the crystal holds 100% and the ghost 0%. On the def316l phantom (1 deg grid): main population median error 0.49 -> 0.19 deg, Sigma3 ghost occupancy 8% -> 4%, twin-lamella voxels wrong 51% -> 20% (interior 49% -> 12%), deviance -6%, 35 s on a GPU. On an Ag2S scan the lamellae sharpen (a lamella grain wins 49% -> 75% of the voxels ImageD11's back-projections give it, 9 min on a GPU for 92k voxels). Each voxel's candidates are its own orientations' children: a ranking over every orientation, as the fit does, lets a grain's many children crowd out a thin twin's. `anri.index.search.predictions` predicts long lists in chunks of one shape (`chunk`), for a local search's millions of orientations.
- `python -m anri.calib`: calibrate the detector and beam geometry on a scanning-3DXRD scan of an indexed sample, as ImageD11's silicon notebook does for a single rotation, but on a whole scan and any number of grains (`anri.calib.stages.stage_fit`, `<...>_calib.h5`; `pars` writes ImageD11 parameter files, `show` the provenance). Peaks are ImageD11's peaks table merged per 4D label and dty row (`anri.io.imaged11.read_peaks`; `read_dataset` returns the DataSet's `pksfile`). Each is matched to an orientation of an `anri.index` fit and a reflection, and scatters from where the beam crosses that orientation's voxels at its dty and omega (`anri.calib.fit.match`): the parallax a single-grain fit cannot model on a scan. The distance, beam centre, three tilts and the wavelength (a calibrant) or the lattice scale (any other sample; the two are degenerate) are fitted by Levenberg-Marquardt to the peaks' (sc, fc, omega), with each orientation's rotation eliminated by a Schur complement (`fit_geometry`, `calibrate`); wedge, chi and y0 stay fixed. `strain_left` then frees each orientation's lattice with the geometry fixed, the strain left measuring the calibration. On the Ni phantom, from a wavelength 1.8% off, a distance 1% off, the centre 3 px and the tilts 2 mrad off, it returns to the truth within 5e-6 (distance and wavelength) and 2 sigma (centre, tilts). On an undeformed Si cube (S3DXRD, 140 um crystal 290 um off the axis), from the wavelength 1.78% off, it leaves 0.2e-5 hydrostatic and at most 2e-5 deviatoric strain, where the geometry from ImageD11's notebook loop leaves -96e-5 and 29e-5 (wavelength 0.18982 against 0.18927 A, distance 150.77 against 151.10 mm).

- `python -m anri.index powder`: exports a dataset's sparse pixels, summed over every frame, as a powder pattern (`anri.index.stages.stage_powder`; an export, not a stage of the indexer). Pixels are read as the indexer reads them and binned by their centre in (2theta, eta); each bin is divided by the solid angle of the measured pixels in it (`anri.index.data.detector_cake`, with `pixel_solid_angle`; the detector mask removes gaps), giving the intensity per sr per frame. `anri.index.data.powder_pattern` gives its esd from the spread over eta (grain statistics: on an Ag2S scan 47 x the counting esd), each ring's measured share of its solid angle (`coverage`), and the share of measured pixel-frames holding a data pixel (`lit_fraction`), which marks the censored valleys between peaks where nearly every pixel was below the segmentation cut. - `python -m anri.index` models the detector mask: the histogram stage reads the DataSet's segmenter mask (`--detector-mask` to give another; ImageD11's convention, non-zero for a masked pixel, `anri.io.imaged11.read_mask`; `read_dataset` returns its `maskfile`) and records the measured share of each (ring, eta bin) (`anri.index.data.detector_coverage`: the detector's pixels sampled 4 x 4 after distortion, within each ring's tolerance; also 0 where a ring leaves the detector), as `coverage` in the rings group. The search's completeness leaves out predictions on less than half-measured bins, and the filter's and the voxel fit's system matrices scale each prediction by its bin's coverage, so a reflection on a module gap is no longer taken as a missing grain. On an Ag2S scan (Eiger, 72 keV) the horizontal module gaps hid every ring at eta +-(65-86) deg, about 10% of the predictions, which the fit had read as zeros. Histogram files from before carry no coverage, and are fitted as before.
- One voxel grid for the whole pipeline: the histogram stage fixes it (`n` x `n` voxels of the row step, centred on the rotation axis, sized from the DataSet's y0) and writes it as a `grid` group, which `load_histogram` returns and the fit and populations stages use; a y0 chosen later moves no voxel. `--grid-pad` (histogram stage, sample units) widens it on every side, for a sample reaching past the scanned range, whose outer part would otherwise pile its intensity onto the grid's edge (on an Ag2S sample 50 um past the 417-voxel grid: the edge pile-up gone and the deviance 1.3% lower). `anri.geom.scan.regrid` moves an array between two grids centred on the axis (the same voxels, cropped or padded alike on every side). Masks carry their voxel size (`anri.index.mask.save_mask`, an .npz) and `load_mask` centres one onto the grid; a bare .npy, e.g. an ImageD11 mask, is taken to have voxels of the grid's size. The populations stage's TensorMap holds each voxel's sample-frame position (`sx`, `sy`, and `sz` = 0 for a slice).
- `python -m anri.index --saturation 65535` (histogram stage): pixels whose raw counts reached the detector's saturation (`anri.io.imaged11.stream_sparse(saturation=...)` flags them before any normalisation) make their bins lower bounds in the fit: where the model predicts at least the data there it agrees, below it the data pull it up (`anri.index.occupancy.censored_ratio` and `deviance` take the saturated bins' indices; the histogram stage stores them as `H_saturated`). Clipped peaks otherwise pull the occupancies, and absorption, down where spots are brightest.
- `python -m anri.index` models absorption whenever the histogram stage had a `--cif` (or `--mu` is given; `--no-absorption` to turn it off): each system entry is scaled by the transmission along the incoming and the diffracted path of that voxel and prediction through the sample, whose shape is the fit's mask (`anri.index.occupancy.system` takes positions followed by each voxel's edge distances; `fit_occupancy(absorb=(sample_map, step))` builds them, also for the coarse voxels of `--coarse`). The occupancies are then free of absorption, and the fitted model has it. A disc is the wrong shape unless the sample fills it: give the sample's mask. The fit stage records mu in its provenance. The filter stage's global orientation fit has no voxel positions and stays without absorption.
- Absorption, the parts below the methods: `anri.crystal.structure.attenuation(structure, energy_kev)` gives the linear attenuation coefficient (1/um) from a structure's composition and density (Dans_Diffraction's tables, which reproduce NIST XCOM); `anri.geom.paths` gives the path through the sample from each voxel to its edge. `edge_distances` builds a table `D[voxel, phi]` over the in-plane directions (the sample a prism of a 2D map; for each direction, the map resampled along it and summed backwards: 0.5 s on a GPU for 174k voxels x 360 directions), `ray_directions` the incoming and diffracted directions in the sample frame, `path_lengths` the path along each (`D / cos(elevation)`), and `transmission` exp(-mu (L_in + L_out)). Ag2S at 72 keV (mu = 2.2 /mm) transmits only 10-30% through the middle of a 0.8 mm sample.
- `python -m anri.index --pad` grows the disc mask (sample units): a sample a little larger than the disc the beam sees at every omega is fitted whole, instead of its outer part's intensity piling onto the disc's edge (seen as smeared occupancy where the disc clips a corner of the sample). A voxel at radius r is in the beam over (2/pi) asin(r_disc / r) of the rotation, 2/3 at 1.15 r_disc.

- Fly and helical scans, where dty moves while omega turns: `anri.io.read_frame_dty(ds)` gives each frame's dty, the DataSet's (corrected) dty of its row plus the motion within the row from the sparse file's raw readings (a straight line over the row's frames). `anri.index.dty_offsets` turns it into each row's offset from its nominal dty per omega bin, and `anri.index.system` (`scan["ddty"]`) and `backproject` put each voxel in the rows that really saw it. `python -m anri.index` does this by default when dty moves (`--no-frame-dty` to ignore it); step scans are unchanged. On a helical scan (one dty step per turn) the rows' frames are up to half a step off the row's mean dty. `--dty-source frames` bins each frame by its own dty instead of its row's: a helical rotation then spreads over 2-3 rows, some (row, omega) bins get no frames and some two rotations' worth (which made the maps' rings and holes), so `dty_offsets(return_exposure=True)` also gives each bin's exposure, which `system` (`scan["exposure"]`) and `reconstruct` scale by. - `anri.index.mlem` is SQUAREM-accelerated MLEM (Varadhan & Roland 2008): each iteration is three MLEM updates and an extrapolation, kept above 1e-3 of the plain update (MLEM multiplies, so an occupancy at 0 could never come back) and never fitting worse than plain MLEM. `--iter` now counts these iterations, default 10 (was 50 plain): on the def316l phantom 10 iterations fit twin lamellae better than 50 plain ones did (48% of their voxels wrong, against 52%), in two-thirds of the time, with the untwinned voxels' mean orientation 0.02 deg further from the truth (a converged fit puts a grain's occupancy on fewer grid points); 5 iterations match the old default in 40% of the time. `mlem(callback=...)` reports each iteration (the notebook records its plots with it). It holds at most four copies of the occupancies.
- `python -m anri.index --beam FWHM`: each voxel is spread over the dty rows by the beam's profile across dty (a Gaussian of that FWHM, integrated over the voxel at each omega, as `anri.fwd.beam_weight`), over as many rows as it reaches (`anri.index.beam_rows`), instead of linearly over 2 rows. Default 0: the 2-row model, as before. `anri.index.system` takes `n_beam` (the fifth element of `dims`) and the beam in `scan` (`sig_beam`, `width_beam`, `voxel`); `block_voxels` takes the entries per prediction. With FWHM = step the two models are close: on the def316l phantom and on a real AP1_1 scan (scored by how well the maps explain the pixels) the maps are as good either way, and the profile costs ~2.4x in the candidate pass. It is for beams wider than the step (overfocusing), where a voxel reaches more rows.
- `python -m anri.index --censor C`: ImageD11 keeps only pixels above its segmentation cut, so an empty histogram bin had every pixel below the cut. Where the model predicts fewer than C counts in an empty bin, it counts as agreeing with the model, in both MLEMs (`anri.index.censored_ratio`) and in the deviance and the pruning's likelihood ratio (`anri.index.deviance`). ### Changed

- The global orientation fit of `python -m anri.index filter` (`anri.index.orientations.fit`) is SQUAREM-accelerated MLEM, as the voxel fit: `n_iter` counts SQUAREM iterations (3 MLEM updates each), default 20 (was 20 plain updates). Under plain MLEM, decoys that borrow intensity from real grains faded slowly: on AP1_1, 20 updates left likelihood ratios down to -5e5 (removing those orientations would have improved the fit), 20 SQUAREM iterations -6e3, in 24 s against 12 s. With `--cand 256` after it, the voxel fit's deviance is 1% lower and 58-63 voxels inside the sample are empty instead of 67. SQUAREM's step length depends on the GPU's summation order, so runs on the same input differ by 20-50 of ~24,000 orientations at a ratio of 1 (up to ~70 in ratios below 100). Both fits run one SQUAREM loop, `anri.index.occupancy.squarem(step, f, n_iter)`, each with its own MLEM update.
- The beam profile is in `anri.geom.beam`, beside `beam_basis` and `beam_offsets`: `beam_weight` (was `anri.fwd.render.beam_weight`) and `chord_weight`, public now, which the indexer's system uses instead of a private function of the renderer.

Each file records its provenance as HDF5 attributes (`provenance/`, `options`, `resolved`, `results`): the stage, a unique id, its source file and that file's id (checked when read, so a remade or replaced upstream file is refused), the anri version, git commit, date, working directory and command. - `python -m anri.render`: simulate a scanning-3DXRD dataset from a map, in three stages with provenance, like the indexer. `prepare` turns a map (an ImageD11 TensorMap with any number of phases, or the indexer's `_entries.npz`) and a geometry (`.par` or `pars.json`) into each phase's map entries, reflections (out to the largest ring wholly on the detector) and structure factors (`--cif PATH`, or `--cif NAME=PATH` per phase), the beam's spreads (ID11 presets: `--optics al-crl` or `si-nanolens` for the convergence, the Laue monochromator's 2e-3 bandwidth; or numbers) and the scan's motor positions (rows covering the sample plus a margin). `render [--rows a:b]` renders rows into sparse pixels above `--cut`, every phase summed, so rows can be split between jobs; `assemble` gathers the parts made from a prepare file (checking every row is there once) into an ImageD11 dataset (`_sparse.h5`, `pars/`, the DataSet; `--peaks-table`) that `python -m anri.index` reads, and records how it was made in `_assemble.h5`. `run` does all three, `show` prints the chain. The indexer's `_entries.npz` also holds the voxel size, lattice, space group and phase name.
- Provenance of stage files is in `anri.io`, for every method: `write_provenance`, `write_results`, `read_provenance`, `source_of` (the file a stage file was made from, its id checked), `chain`, `upstream`, `show`, `stage_path` (a stage's default output name) and `write_attrs`/`read_attrs`. The chain is followed by each file's source, not by a list of the method's stages. `anri.index` no longer exports `read_provenance`, `show` and `upstream`.
- `python -m anri.index --mask` defaults to `disc`: only the voxels the beam sees at every omega, within the scanned dty range all round the rotation axis (`anri.index.disc_mask`), are fitted. The square grid's outer voxels are in the beam at some omegas only, so the data hardly constrain them, and MLEM gave them large occupancies that explained almost nothing: on a small two-grain simulation without a mask, the outer voxels' total occupancy was ~7x the sample's, `--occupied` (relative to the 99th percentile) chose them and dropped the whole sample. `--mask auto` (the Otsu threshold, largest region and convex hull) now lies within the disc too. `stage_fit(mask=None)` still fits every voxel. `anri.index.plots.plot_mask` outlines the disc.
- The numbers and plots behind the choice of each of `python -m anri.index`'s options are in anri, not in a notebook: `anri.index.diagnostics` (pure functions of a dataset or of the stage files: `ring_profile_cake` and `ring_windows`, `row_profile`, `y0_from_edges`, `back_projection`, `lit_curve`, `eta_profile`, `grid_chances`, `grid_deviances`, `nearest_stronger`, `convergence_recorder` (a callback for `stage_fit`), `candidate_shares`, `ring_ratios`) and `anri.index.plots` (matplotlib, imported only there: each `plot_x(fig, ...)` draws on a figure it is given and returns `update(**options) -> str`, which redraws for other values of the options and summarises them in a line, so Jupyter sliders or a workflow widget can drive it; the selections are the stages' own). `indexing_parameters.ipynb` is rewritten on them and on the stages (from 1081 lines of code to 285). `anri.index.run_command(file)`: the `python -m anri.index run` command that remakes a stage file and those before it, with every option they record. `--min-comp` moves from `search` to `filter`, where it is chosen, so it can change without redoing the search: the search file keeps every completeness and its chance and halfway levels (`anri.index.completeness_levels`), and the filter makes the cut (`select_by_completeness`). `stage_fit(save_model=True)` also saves the fitted histogram. `anri.index.cake` and `pixel_cake` (a (2θ, η) cake of a stream of pixels) replace `tth_profile`; `grid_chance` is one grid step's chance completeness, as `choose_grid` draws it; `select_occupied` is the `--occupied` cut. Results unchanged (bit-identical).
- `anri.maths`, the layer underneath the rest of anri (JAX and NumPy only). `anri.maths.rotation` holds all rotation algebra: `rot_x`, `rot_y`, `rot_z`, `skew` and `rmat_from_axis_angle` (were in `anri.geom`), `quat_to_mat`, `mat_to_quat`, `quat_mul`, `rod_to_mat`, `mat_to_rod`, `rotation_angle` and `mean_rotation` (were in `anri.crystal`), and `random_rotations` and `small_rotations` (were in `anri.phantom`). `anri.maths.utils` holds `inv3`, `fwhm_to_sigma`, `sigma_to_fwhm` (were in `anri.utils`) and `float64` (was in `anri.crystal`). `anri.crystal` keeps what needs crystal symmetry (Laue groups, disorientations, fundamental zones, grids) and builds on it; `anri.utils` keeps `setup` and `mesh`. The old names are gone, not aliased. Outputs unchanged.
- One (2theta, eta) convention, in `anri.diffract`: `k_out_to_tth_eta(k_out, k_in_lab)` (any beam direction; the indexer's private `_tth_eta`), with `tth_eta_to_k_out` its inverse and `q_lab_to_tth_eta`'s eta taken from it. Both take an optional `k_in_lab` (default: along lab x, as before).
- `anri.fwd.make_row` also gives each row's middle omega and dty (`omega_mid`, `dty_mid`), which the renderer used to recompute in five places.
- One rotation construction, in `anri.geom`: `skew(v)` (the cross-product matrix) and `rmat_from_axis_angle(axis, angle)`, now batched (Rodrigues' formula without a matrix product). `anri.crystal.rod_to_mat` and the renderer's orientation spread build on `skew`; `anri.phantom.axis_angle` is gone (use `anri.geom.rmat_from_axis_angle`). Faster: axis-angle 1.9x on CPU for 1e5 rotations (was `jax.scipy` `Rotation`), `rod_to_mat` 4-9x on CPU and 1.5-2.5x on GPU, the phantom's host rotations 5x; renders unchanged (bit-identical, same time).
- One source for orientation averages and angles in `anri.crystal`: `rotation_angle` (from the axial part and the trace, via atan2), `closest_equivalent` (a symmetric equivalent nearest a reference, and the angle) and `mean_rotation` (weighted SVD projection, always a proper rotation). `disorientation`, `to_fundamental_zone`, `anri.index.populations` and `anri.phantom.tensormap` use them. Population spreads lose a float32 noise floor: arccos of the trace quantised every spread below ~0.03 degrees (single-orientation populations reported 0.028 degrees; now ~1e-6). `phantom.tensormap`'s grain means no longer risk an improper matrix (a missing determinant fix), and its misorientations of float32 maps are accurate to ~1e-5 degrees instead of ~0.03.
- Lorentz and polarisation live in `anri.diffract`: `lorentz` and `polarisation` (moved from `anri.fwd`), and `lorentz_polarisation(k_in, k_out, wedge, chi, pol_factor)`, the per-reflection product the renderer and the indexer each computed themselves. `anri.index.predict` returns it with each prediction, (eta, omega, valid, lp), so the Ewald condition is solved once; `anri.index.lorentz_polarisation` is gone. Outputs bit-identical.
- `anri.geom.recon_to_sample`: reconstruction indices to sample positions (`recon_to_step`, then `step_to_sample`), used by `recon_positions` and `anri.io.entries_from_tensormap` (which called ImageD11's). `anri.index.fit_occupancy(coarse=...)` builds its coarse grid with `recon_positions` instead of its own meshgrid: the same positions, in ImageD11's order. Fine voxels midway between coarse voxels have no unique 9 nearest, so which equally near neighbours they inherit candidates from can change, and coarse runs differ slightly.
- `anri.crystal.U_B_to_UBI`: the one (U B)^-1, used by the indexer's predictions and results and by `anri.phantom.tensormap` (each inverted U @ B itself). The indexer's predictions now use the explicit 3x3 inverse (`anri.utils.inv3`); results agree to float32 rounding.
- `anri.geom.wrap_omega` (into [om0, om0 + 360)) and `nearest_omega` (the turn nearest a reference): the indexer's histogram binning, `dty_offsets` and the renderer each wrapped omega inline. `dty_offsets` now bins omega exactly as `anri.index.system` does, so its offsets always line up with the histogram's omega bins.
- `anri.geom.dty_to_row`: the one frame-to-row assignment, used by `anri.io.stream_sparse` and `anri.index.dty_offsets` (which had separate copies that had to agree).
- `anri.utils.fwhm_to_sigma` and `sigma_to_fwhm`: the one Gaussian FWHM conversion. `python -m anri.index` used both 2.355 and 2 sqrt(2 ln 2); it now uses the exact factor everywhere, so its default beam and bandwidth widths change by 0.008%.
- One forward-model centroid, one scattering origin. `anri.fwd.get_centroid` (and `get_centroid_both`) projects (ubi, hkl) to (sc, fc, omega) with the voxel at its real position for a given dty; the renderer uses it instead of its own copy, `get_centroid_box` is it at dty = y0 for a cube, and `get_centroid_scan` (which solves for the dty that centres the voxel on a pencil beam) shares its origin. `anri.geom.origin_lab` is where a voxel scatters (a column raised to a tilted beam's height), used by `get_centroid` and `dty_and_origin_lab`; `anri.geom.beam_offsets` is a point's position relative to the beam's centre line, used by `origin_lab` and `anri.fwd.beam_weight`. Outputs are unchanged: renders, peak selection, and scan and box centroids and covariances are bit-identical, except for tilted beams (differences ~1e-15 relative, from the order of operations).
- `python -m anri.index`: `--iter` defaults to 50 MLEM iterations (was 10). Features 1-2 voxels thin, such as twin lamellae, are still converging long after the deviance flattens: at 10 iterations a twin lost to its parent in voxels it fills. On a phantom, more iterations keep improving the map (pure voxels wrong: 0.36% at 10, 0.22% at 30, 0.14% at 100).
- `anri.index.histogram_pixels` takes a list of `((b_e, b_o, n_e, n_o), n_rows)` and fills all of them in one pass over the data. `python -m anri.index` reads the sparse pixels once again (it read them twice, which doubled the slowest step on large scans), and preparing each chunk is cheaper.
- Tests run on CPU by default, as in CI (`conftest.py` sets `JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu` unless it is set): on a GPU they mostly waited for XLA:GPU to compile small one-off shapes.
- `anri.fwd.render_peaks` weights each frame by the beam at that frame's own omega (the mean omega of the peak's mass within the frame), not at the peak's centroid omega. A peak spread in omega (e.g. by `"sig_rot"`) diffracts at different omegas, where a voxel off the rotation axis sits at a different place across the beam, so its intensity spreads over dty rows along the voxel's sinusoid. For a 0.5 degree spread 50 um off the axis with a 0.25 um beam, the intensity per row was off by ~150% and is now within ~6% of explicitly rotated sub-entries. `select_peaks` widens its reach across the beam by the voxel's motion within the omega margin. Sharp peaks are essentially unchanged.
- `anri.fwd.render_peaks` centres each frame's pixel window on the peak's mean position in that frame (it used to centre every frame on the peak's overall centroid), so a peak that moves across the detector with omega stays inside its window. Peaks within one or two frames are unchanged.
- `anri.fwd.render_row` is faster on GPUs: duplicate pixels (neighbouring voxels light up the same pixels, ~100x for a grain) are summed on the device, so only unique pixels go to the host, and batches are 1024 peaks per device times a power of 4, so a scan compiles at most a few shapes (XLA:GPU took up to a minute to compile very small batches). A 153-row scan of a 6818-voxel grain renders in 1 minute instead of 3. Output is unchanged up to float rounding; CPUs keep the host merge, as XLA's CPU sort is slower than NumPy's.
- For a beam tilted out of the horizontal plane, the scattering origin in the scanning model is where the pencil crosses the voxel's column: the beam is taken to cross the rotation axis at the voxel's own height (the layer's height in a 2D map), so the origin is raised by (k_z / k_x) x. Nothing changes for a horizontal beam.
- The renderer handles pencil, line and box beams (e.g. DCT) with one model. Every voxel sits at its real lab position for the row's dty (it used to be moved onto the pencil's centre line), and `anri.fwd.beam_weight` (replacing `dty_weight`) integrates the beam's profile across it over the voxel: horizontally and vertically, each a flat top blurred by a Gaussian (`geom_from_pars(..., sig_beam, width_beam, sig_beam_v, width_beam_v)`). Voxels are columns for 2D maps or cubes for 3D maps (`voxel_3d`). The beam can point anywhere (`k_in_lab`): it sees the voxel rotated by omega - psi, and travels 1/cos(alpha) further through a column. `select_peaks` keeps peaks whose voxel is within reach of the beam.
- `anri.fwd.polarisation(k_in, k_out, factor)` takes the beam direction: horizontal polarisation is across the beam, wherever it points.

### Fixed

- `anri.index.stages.run_command` on a chain with a fit but no local search gives `--levels 0`: `run` would otherwise add a local search the files never had.
- `anri.maths.rotation.rmat_from_axis_angle` with an integer axis (e.g. `[0, 0, 1]`) no longer truncates the angle to whole degrees: an integer axis is taken as floating point ([#34](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/34)).
- `python -m anri.simulate run`: the positional output dataset name and `--dataset` (an ImageD11 DataSet to simulate) shared one argparse destination, so without `--dataset` the run tried to open the output name as an input DataSet, and with it the output was named after the input path. They are now separate ([#25](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/25)).
- A stage given an output file that is one of its inputs (`--out` naming its source, a file further up the chain, or the DataSet, map or mask it reads) erased it: the file was opened for writing before anything else. Every stage of `anri.index` and `anri.simulate` now refuses such an output first (`anri.io.provenance.check_out`, also through links), and the input is kept ([#26](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/26)).
- `anri.index.occupancy.fit_occupancy` failed in `top_k` when fewer orientations were kept than the candidates asked for (`--cand`, default 64): it now takes as many candidates as there are orientations, and refuses an empty list with a clear message ([#32](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/32)).
- `anri.geom.scan.step_grid_from_ybincens` with `gridstep > 1` could leave 0 off the grid (e.g. -17, -15, ..., 17), so the grid was no longer centred where `step_to_recon` puts the rotation axis. The half extent is now rounded up to whole grid steps, as ImageD11 does after its release 2.1.3 ([#33](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/33)).
- `anri.fwd.render` compiled a new slice for every count of rendered pixels it copied back from a device, which changes from batch to batch. The slice is now rounded up to a power of 2 (at most twice the pixels needed) and cut on the host, so a scan compiles a few lengths instead of one per batch ([#35](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/35)).
- `anri.index.data.detector_coverage` counted only the part of a ring's window on the detector, so a (ring, eta bin) running off the detector's edge read as fully covered, but as part covered if masked pixels extended the detector. It is now also scaled by the share of the bin's window (2theta within the tolerance, eta within the bin) that reaches the detector. Histogram files made before keep their coverage: remake them to use it ([#30](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/30)).
- `anri.index.populations.group` left out candidates below 2% of a voxel's total, so a voxel whose occupancy was spread thinly over many orientations had no population, yet was exported with the identity orientation. The cut `eps` is now relative to the voxel's most occupied candidate: such a voxel comes out as one population with a large spread ([#27](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/27)).
- `anri.index.populations.group` returned populations in the order they were seeded, not largest first as documented, so the main population (column 0, used by the TensorMap) could be the smaller one. They are now sorted by fraction ([#29](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/29)).
- `python -m anri.index populations` exported each population's rms misorientation as `sig_rot`, which the renderer reads as the standard deviation of each component of the rotation: renders from `_entries.npz` were sqrt(3) too wide. It now exports the spread / sqrt(3) ([#31](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/31)).
- `python -m anri.index --coarse`: a fine voxel's candidates from its coarse neighbourhood (`anri.index.occupancy.inherit_candidates`) ranked an orientation found in several neighbours by whichever copy sorted first, so a strong one could be dropped for a weak copy, and repeated an orientation when the neighbourhood held fewer than 2k. An orientation now ranks by its largest occupancy in any neighbour, comes once, and empty slots are -1, which `candidates_from` leaves out ([#28](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/28)).
- `python -m anri.index filter` (`anri.index.orientations.fit`) kept a different set of orientations each run on a GPU. The likelihood ratio summed `d log(mu / (mu - g a))` in float32: for a weak orientation, mu / (mu - g a) rounds to 1 plus a few ulps, and times ~1e6 counts per row-summed bin that is noise of order 1 in the ratio. The GPU's summation order changes the occupancies by ~1e-6 from run to run, and the noise turned that into 0.1-5 in the ratio: on AP1_1, 725 orientations crossed `--min-lr 1` between two runs on the same input. It now uses `log1p`: with plain MLEM, two runs then kept the same orientations at 1, 25 and 100 (with SQUAREM, see Changed, 20-50 of 24,000 still differ). The maps hardly depend on it (orientations below a ratio of 100 held no occupancy there).
- `anri.index.system`, `backproject` and `reconstruct` put a voxel in the dty rows with a beam along lab x and an untilted rotation axis, whatever the geometry: they ignored wedge, chi and the beam's direction. They now use `anri.geom.find_dty_for_beam_xy` (now broadcasting over points and omegas, elementwise, so it is cheap on the indexer's hot path), with optional scan keys "k_in_lab", "wedge" and "chi" (default: untilted, along x); `python -m anri.index` passes its geometry's. With a beam profile the voxel's chord is taken at omega - psi, as in `anri.fwd.beam_weight`. Untilted geometries are unchanged.
- `anri.index.fit_occupancy(coarse=...)` (`python -m anri.index --coarse`) on fly and helical scans: the coarse pass kept the fine rows' `ddty` and `exposure`, so coarse row k used fine row k's dty offsets. The new `anri.index.coarsen_scan` gives the coarse scan of rows summed in groups (also used for `--gridstep`), with each group's mean exposure and mean real dty. Step scans are unchanged.
- `anri.fwd` (`bin_fractions`, `truncated_moments`, so every rendered pixel): a Gaussian's mass in a bin far in its upper tail, Phi(b) - Phi(a), cancelled in float32 to about 1e-7 of either sign (2% off at 5 sigma, 0 instead of 5e-10 at 6 sigma). Times a bright peak's amplitude, and summed over the many voxels sharing a pixel, it made pixels far from peaks wrong, even negative (a log-likelihood of them was NaN). The upper tail now uses Phi(-a) - Phi(-b), and a mass is never negative.
- `python -m anri.index`: the measured / fitted intensity per dty row (`row_ratio`) now sums only the eta bins the fit models. It included the data at |sin eta| <= `--etacut`, which the fit does not predict, so every row read too high.
- `anri.io.write_pars` writes `t_x`, `t_y`, `t_z` (0) and `omegasign` (1) when the geometry lacks them: ImageD11 needs them to compute peak geometry (`DataSet.update_colfile_pars` raised a KeyError on simulated datasets).
- `anri.index.inherit_candidates` finds each voxel's coarse neighbours with a KD-tree: sorting every distance took ~2 minutes on a 419 × 419 map, more than the candidate pass that `--coarse` saves (now ~4 s).
- `python -m anri.index` writes IPF (`ipf_x/y/z`) and Euler maps into its TensorMap again, and a ParaView `.xdmf` beside it, as the sandbox script did. Strain maps are left out: these UBIs are rotations of the nominal lattice, so the strain would be exactly zero.
- `anri.fwd.render_row` with `"sig_rot"` failed under newer JAX (shard_map's check that stacked arrays vary across the same devices): the rotation's cross-product matrix is now a sum over constant generators.
- Wrong spot positions in float32 on recent NVIDIA GPUs: JAX's default precision for float32 matrix products there is TF32 (10-bit mantissa), which moved rendered spots ~1000 px from the beam centre by up to 0.3 px on an L40S, a strain error of ~1e-5. `anri.utils.setup()` now sets `jax_default_matmul_precision` to `"highest"` (unless `JAX_DEFAULT_MATMUL_PRECISION` is set). GPU renders made in float32 without this fix should be redone.
- Beam divergence (`ky`, `kz`) was applied along lab y and z, which is only across the beam when it is along lab x: for a tilted beam the vertical divergence shrank by cos(tilt). It is now applied along the beam's own horizontal and vertical (`anri.geom.beam_basis`), in every forward model.
- `anri.geom.step_grid_from_ybincens` always raised: it was jitted, but the size of its grid depends on its inputs.
- `render_peaks` put up to ~3% of a peak's intensity in the wrong cells (and lost up to ~7% from broad, truncated peaks): when conditioning fast on slow it held omega at its frame mean, ignoring that slow and omega are correlated within the frame. It now integrates omega out within the frame. Errors against a Monte Carlo of the beam spreads went from 1% (median worst cell) to 0.13%, the Monte Carlo noise.
- Wrong renders on CPU with jaxlib >= 0.11: XLA:CPU's YNNPACK fusions miscompiled `render_peaks` for batches of more than a few thousand peaks (in float64, most peaks squeezed into one pixel; in float32, NaNs). `anri.utils.setup()` now turns them off with `--xla_cpu_experimental_ynn_fusion_type=`. CPU renders made with jaxlib 0.11.x without this fix should be redone.
- `anri.crystal.__all__` and `anri.fwd.__all__` listed functions that don't exist, so `from anri.crystal import *` failed. `anri.fwd` now exports `propagate_cov_scan_both`.

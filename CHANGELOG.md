## Unreleased

### Added

- The fit stage proves its map is the maximum likelihood over every orientation of the last level, or says where it is not (`stage_fit(price_rounds=1, price_eps=1e-4)`, `fit --price-rounds`, `--price-eps`; `anri.index.occupancy.price`, `fit_occupancy(price_rounds=, price_eps=)`). The likelihood is concave (the censored and saturated bins' Poisson tails are log-concave), so a fit converged on its candidates is the full problem's optimum iff no other orientation's score A^T rho / A^T 1 exceeds 1 (`ls-concave`, `ls-kkt`). One pass over every fine voxel and orientation, the cost of a candidate pass, prices them; violators take new slots (each round adds 8 per voxel, so nothing the fit uses is pushed out, and the slots are cut back at the end) and the fit resumes. An orientation's score bounds what it could gain, 2 (S - 1) per photon (`ls-gain`), and the log gives the rigorous bound on the distance from the optimum for plain Poisson bins (`ls-gap`). This is what fixes the crowding of near-duplicate orientations: they score ~1 at the fit (an exact duplicate of a fitted one exactly 1), where the first update from a uniform start ranks them with the grain they copy. Pricing treats light the model leaves unexplained as an infinite score (MLEM's ratio is 0 there). The MLEM convergence test now needs two iterations running (the first after a start sees only the fast modes). The indexing parameters guide sets and explains `--tol`, `--price-rounds` and `--price-eps`, and no longer recommends stopping early; the convergence callback records the fit before pricing only.

- The indexer's MLEM (voxel fit and global orientation fit) stops when it converges: each SQUAREM iteration's two plain updates give the deviance still to fall, delta_1 / (2 (1 - rho)) with rho^2 = delta_1 / delta_0 and delta = 2 sum (A^T 1) df^2 / f the update's first-order decrease (a sum of non-negative terms, no cancellation), and the fit stops once that is at most `tol` (default 1, a likelihood ratio no test can tell from the optimum). `--iter` / `n_iter` is now a cap, and a level that reaches it first is logged as not converged (stopping early is a filter). New: `fit --tol`, `stage_fit(tol=)`, `fit_occupancy(tol=)`, `orientations.fit(tol=)`, `occupancy.step_decrease`, `remaining_deviance`, `convergence_message`; `squarem` and `mlem` return `(f, info)`. Theory page: likelihood, `lik-converged`. Cost: one more reduction per update, in the same host transfer as the deviance.

- The no-shape refiner page writes parallax out exactly (`rf-parallax-exact`): the direction seen from the origin is a linear map of the true one, normalised, (I + eps [n]x[e]x) u / |...|, eps = |x_v| / D; to first order a cubic correction whose third-order term is -delta (delta^T A delta), so a first-moment or |g|^2 feature keeps, beyond second order, a remainder of at most eps |k| |l| |delta|^3 whose mean is a third moment of the spread.
- The no-shape refiner page: the runout's harmonics from the second up are fixed by the populations' centroid curves against omega, and its alternation with the linear solve contracts by the squared cosine between the patterns, stalling only on the first harmonic (`rf-runout-fit`); the box half-width that balances the cut's bound against a variance-only tail, falling as N^(-1/5), set before the fit (`rf-box-width`); a stack of slices adds no weak directions, the weak part at a tilted frequency being the Nye component normal to it (`rf-3d`); the detector displacement's common term, (s_r^2 + s_t^2) / rho^2 of |g|^2 (`rf-displacement`); background smooth across the detector, at worst varying with omega. The robustness, moments and limits pages' left-out lists point to these results.
- The no-shape refiner page: background (`rf-background`): a free background slope per region is exactly a shift of the population (in counts, first and second moments of a symmetric box), so it would remove that region's position information; background is instead estimated from the cells outside every box (fixed by the indexer, so unbiased) with a model smooth over many boxes, and its residual slope bounded. The cost of the solve (`rf-cost`): 2F scalar projections per iteration; the ray sums' normal operator is convolution with 1/|z| (symbol 2 pi / |k|), so a ramp preconditioner leaves kappa = 1 / T(k_max)^2, 5.9 when the beam's FWHM is the dty step: 20 iterations to 1e-3, independent of the grid's size.
- The no-shape refiner page: the precision of the Si fit of the omega kernel (`rf-si-fisher`, `rf-si-narrow`): each reflection's intensity costs nothing, the information on its variance is N K / (2 s^3) for a narrow kernel (K = 1.10), so 500 reflections fix the bandwidth-sized width to 0.3-0.9 %; the reflections' positions must be fitted jointly (their cross information with the widths vanishes on average, and unfitted they would raise the floor).
- The no-shape refiner page: (A6), the instrument's omega kernel as a factor of every peak, measured on a single Si rotation with every experiment (an operating condition of the refinement); its variance is linear in three widths (horizontal and vertical convergence, bandwidth), and a lower variance is still a factor (`rf-omega-kernel`); the omega binning bound with a table of its scale (`rf-omega-bound`); the three widths are determined by a Si rotation on at least two rings, and not by one ring whatever the number of reflections (`rf-omega-identify`).
- The no-shape refiner page: a shape-free bound on the group rule's loss under a radially non-increasing point spread (a reflection lemma and the least leak into a neighbour, 0.0124 for the measured point spread), rigorous but weak, at most 3.6e3 photons per cell (`rf-psf-leak`, `rf-group-psf`); omega binning bounded at h/2 per reflection and not modelled, since a model of the omega instrument would be a model of what widens peaks (proposed choice); exact box margins to all orders in the indexer's error, and a strain's turn of g, which the first-order margin had missed (`rf-box-margin-exact`); bounds for the per-cell Lorentz-polarisation division and absorption within a spot (`rf-weight-bound`) and for parallax (`rf-parallax`).
- The no-shape refiner page: runout's first harmonic is a translation of the map (a gauge), Friedel pairs see its even harmonics and none of its odd ones (`rf-runout`); reflections whose box reaches past the omega range are left out; histograms and moments combine as sub-populations per bin, each with its own moments, linear and with a bounded edge term (`rf-bin-moments`).
- The no-shape refiner page: the metric's split has the natural boundary conditions of a free plate edge (zero normal moment and Kirchhoff shear; the biharmonic inside), derived by parts (`rf-plate`); the mean rotation read from the fitted moments differs from the rotations' chordal mean only through rotation-strain correlation (`rf-polar`); diffuse light needs no model of its shape: centrosymmetric light adds nothing to a centred first moment in a box symmetric about the Bragg point, an off-centre box differs by the light of slabs at its faces, which the data hold (`rf-shift`), and second moments include it, so spreads are upper bounds. Boxes that follow arcs: the image of a population's rotation ellipsoid in the tangent plane, widened by the curvature |d|^2/4 (`rf-arc-box`); arcs of many voxels' populations follow by the union of their boxes.
- The no-shape refiner page: the level below which a direction is reported as not determined, per direction from the noise and the misfit bounds (`rf-smin`); histograms over bins along each arc are linear in each population's orientation distribution, with the moments as their linear functionals, and bins a few instrument widths wide need only the instrument's variance (`rf-hist`, `rf-bin-edge`); the seen/weak split on the voxel grid as an exact discrete projector, its boundary conditions implicit in its normal equations (`rf-split-grid`).
- The no-shape refiner page shows it is one linear solve: boxes wider than the indexer's orientation error over |sin eta| (and its eta and strain margins, by Cauchy-Schwarz on the spot's motion) are complete at once, so the regions are fixed from the indexer and never iterated. Two populations of a voxel are told apart only by the reflections that separate them, which must span three dimensions (six for the metric); otherwise their sum is reported. The weak part of the first moments in a merged domain is each row's solenoidal in-plane field (the Nye tensor's z column), split from its gradient part by a Neumann Poisson problem per row; the metric's doubly weak part is Saint-Venant's incompatibility. Two outputs: the weak part fitted and flagged, and the curl-free constraint; their difference is a diagnostic. All proofs symbolic.
- The theory pages cite Henningsson and Hall (2023) for the linear centroid model and its factorisation into scalar projections, and say how the refiner's model differs; and carry a note that, as they were written with the help of a large language model, some citations may be missing at first and are added as they are found.
- The no-shape refiner page derives the weak directions for any statistic: a reflection carrying frequency k sees each population's F acting on k and z, and on z x k only through sin theta (sin^2 theta for the metric's C_xixi), whatever is computed from the photons (pixels, histograms, moments); misfits overlapping a weak pattern are amplified by 1 / s_i (5.5-23 for Ni, 30-530 for C_xixi), and any fitted nuisance lowers s_i. On a voxel grid the weak combinations alias to frequencies beyond the beam's resolution, where the model sees them through its own voxel shape and beam transfer (0.028 at the first alias of a beam one voxel wide): fitted from the model's errors. Features centred on a reference per region make geometry errors (runout, beam profile) misfit by the deformation, not the lattice; estimating only the directions above a level s_min bounds the bias by |Delta| / s_min. The statistic divides by each frame's monitor after the recording.
- Theory page "A refiner that needs no spot shape" (`docs/source/theory/refiner.rst`, proofs in `tests/theory/test_refiner.py`): a population's deformation moments (V, the volume integrals of F, F^T F and F (x) F, any orientation and strain distribution) are exactly linear in sums of recorded values over regions, each cell divided by its own Lorentz and polarisation factors (without that a 1 deg spread turns the light's mean by sigma^2 |cot eta|, 1.5e-3 rad near the axis). The sum of |g|^2 sees strain alone, unaffected by any rotation or by omega binning; the wavelength and the beam's direction add common terms only. The estimator is any left inverse of a geometry-only matrix: noise biases nothing; with a cut no estimator is unbiased, and the cut's bias is bounded per cell by kappa_1 = 1/e; the group rule's by a binomial tail where neighbours are lit, and unboundedly otherwise; the pixels' by the point spread's characteristic function, for any spot of which it is a factor (0.0676 px). Recorded values are non-decreasing in every count, so correlations are non-negative and independence understates error bars; error bars come from residuals over blocks that are independent exactly. The refinement page's design list now points to it.
- Theory page "What a fitted statistic is robust to" (`docs/source/theory/robustness.rst`, proofs in `tests/theory/test_robustness.py`; the lemmas the no-shape refiner page builds on): which statistics a local orientation and strain refiner can fit without bias from errors in the peak shapes. A wrong weight matrix never biases a fit, only its error bars (`ref-bias`), and a free nuisance absorbs the errors that look like it (`ref-profile`); a separated population's location is unbiased under any centrally symmetric shape error, through the cut and the group rule, at the lattice's symmetry points (`ref-symmetric`); between them the pixels alias a wrong shape by up to sum |d chi(2 pi k)| / (pi k) (`ref-alias`): 0.067 px for the measured point spread drawn as one Gaussian of its variance; a spread moves a mean at second order (`ref-spread-mean`), and a first-order spot on a curved ring reads as a strain of -sigma_eta^2 / 2, 1.5e-4 at 1 deg (`ref-ring`); light beyond one face of a region moves a centroid by up to sigma^2 / t for an unknown shape (`ref-tail`); splitting two merged populations by shape is bias-dominated above 50-30000 photons for plausible shape errors at 3-6 own widths apart (`ref-split`). The page ends with the refiner these results allow: moments over model-fixed regions complete for their contributors, separated light alone in its region, merged light as a ray sum.
- `anri.io.imaged11.recorded_moments`: the mean and variance of a cell's recorded value given the expected light in its 5 x 5 neighbourhood (Poisson counts, the cut, the group rule; exact for `pixels_in_spot` <= 3, differentiable), and `neighbourhoods`, which gathers those from sparse cells. Proved against the theory page's animal sums and checked against counts drawn and recorded by `segment`. The recording page gains a table of what the group rule removes from compact spots: under ~100 photons it depends on the spot's sub-pixel position (a sharp 30-photon spot keeps 10 % centred on a pixel, all of it on a corner).

- The renderer reports, per peak, the light beyond each face of its window (`render_row` stats `face_light` [n_peaks, 6]: the scan's frames below and above the window, then over its frames the rows below and above each frame's row window, and within its rows the columns below and above), exactly for the factorised Gaussian (each face's cells telescope to a tail of the conditional the cells are cut from), and for a thick sensor's slices and a mixed point spread as the components' sum by their shares, never extrapolated; `captured + face_light.sum(1) == in_range`. `face_decay` bounds how far beyond a face the light reaches (the light n cells out is at most `face_light * face_decay**n`, from the cells' log-concavity, per Gaussian). Theory page: peak shape (`ps-faces`, `ps-face-sum`, `ps-face-reach`). Cost: two more Gaussian tails per frame and per (frame, row), in the same jitted call (XLA drops them where unused).
- The theory pages (`docs/source/theory`) are Anri's single source of truth for its maths, and every numbered equation is now proved, defined, cited or measured. Proofs are sympy tests in `tests/theory` (173, ~40 s): identities reduced to zero, symbolic integrals and sums, exact ranks; each number quoted in the prose is checked as the exact rounding of its expression at 30 digits; where an anri function computes an equation, a check compares it in float64 with the proved expression within a tolerance derived from its rounding (ulps times the condition), never a loose one. `tests/theory/test_pages.py` fails if an equation has no status or a page names a proof that does not exist. Paragraphs starting "Anri's rule" are requirements for code. New pages: the detector (pixels as box masses and what they cost a centroid, the point spread before pixel integration, the thick sensor, the distortion, splatting), what the detector records (counting, frames, the monitor, the threshold's expected value and covariances with the group rule, the likelihood of a frame), statistics of recorded light (moments over regions as exact ray sums, what second moments see, the bias of data-defined regions, light beyond a window, the intensity gauge), and what the data determine (the Fourier slice, its weak combinations as the dislocations threading the slice, the beam's transfer, separation and localisation, second moments, model error). The existing pages are corrected where they had drifted from the code (anisotropic `sig_rot`, the populations' radius, SQUAREM's refusal test, the local search's row shift) and gain the steps they skipped. sympy is a dev dependency. AGENTS.md says code follows these pages.
- Anisotropic orientation spreads in the renderer: an entry's `sig_rot` may be a [3, 3] covariance of its small sample-frame rotation vector (radians^2), per entry [N, 3, 3] or shared [3, 3], besides the isotropic standard deviation [N] (unchanged, same cost). It enters as J S J^T, linear in S (any symmetric S), and matches entries rotated explicitly by draws with that covariance to < 1% of what the spread adds (tested with off-diagonal S, which an isotropic spread cannot test). `anri.fwd.peak.make_propagator(diagonal=False)` now takes `out_elems` (it raised before).
- `geom["spatial"]` in the renderer (`anri.fwd.render`): the detector's distortion maps (dx, dy) as `anri.io.imaged11.read_spatial` reads them (the raw pixel (row, col) is at (row + dy, col + dx)). Each peak, computed on the ideal detector, is rendered on the raw pixels a measured detector records: its centroid moved to the raw pixel whose corrected position it is (Newton on the bilinearly interpolated map, exact in one step where the map is affine), its detector covariance and its omega covariances mapped by the inverse of the map's local Jacobian, and the thick-sensor kernel's direction likewise. Exact for a map that is affine over a peak's window: an affine map gives the peak's Gaussian of the equivalent skewed detector (tested), a constant one the same pixels as a moved detector. The point spread is taken as a length on the sensor (isotropic in corrected pixels). Off by default.
- `python -m anri.index fit --y0 pairs --drift pairs` (`stage_fit(y0="pairs", drift="pairs")`, maths in `anri.index.drift`): y0 and the sample's drift against the rotation axis from the Friedel pairs of a full turn, before any indexing. Each reflection at (dty, omega, eta) is seen again at (2 y0 - dty, omega + 180, 180 - eta), in another row, measured at another time; per row, the shift between the two, a + b sin(omega) + c cos(omega), is fitted by Gauss-Newton (`friedel_shifts`). Its constant gives y0, and its sin and cos parts the drift's part odd about the time row y0 was measured (`from_pairs`). `roughness` checks each row's own value (its row-to-row change against its noise, and the rows where it jumps; logged, with a warning); the fit follows the drift smoothed over 31 rows (`measure`, `smooth`), added to the rows' offsets (`scan["ddty"]`). Not each row's own value: the fit takes a voxel's offset from its nearest row, exact only for offsets that change little between rows, and per-row noise of a quarter of a row made row sensitivities wrong by ~10% (measured / fitted per row 0.56-1.93). The fit file keeps the drift (`drift_s`, `drift_c`, the rows' own `drift_s_row`, `drift_c_row`, `drift_err`, `drift_measured`) and the provenance its summary. On Renk_a of ihma936 (NSCOPE, 0.2 um steps, 901 rows): y0 0.337 against the DataSet's 0.368, and a drift of +-0.37 um (cos omega) from the first rows to the last, smooth to its noise; following it removes the shift between data and model the fit's residuals showed (+-0.5 um at the first and last rows, now < 0.08), lowers the deviance by 1.3% and the rms of measured / fitted per row from 0.025 to 0.014, and cuts the occupied voxels with a second population >= 0.1 from 38% to 36%.
- `anri.index.plots.plot_drift`: the drift against the row (time), each row's own and the fit's, with the rows where it jumps, and each row's constant (y0). `anri.index.diagnostics.back_projection(ddty=...)` and `anri.index.mask.shift_rows`: projections resampled to where their rows really were before a full turn's halves are joined (a fly scan's offsets were dropped there before). The indexing-parameters guide's section 3 starts from the Friedel pairs (`y0 = "pairs"`, `drift = "pairs"`) and makes its threshold mask with the drift.
- The histogram stage writes the whole sample's sinogram at the frames' omega step (at least 0.1 deg), rings and eta summed: `H_sino` [omega, row] in the file, with its step, read by `load_histogram` (`d["H_sino"]`, `d["sino_omega_step"]`). It is made in the same pass as the other histograms (2896 x 541 values for a 0.125 deg, 541-row scan).
- `python -m anri.calib detector` (`anri.calib.stages.stage_detector`, maths in `anri.calib.detector`): fits the detector's point spread, a core and a wing, to isolated spots in a scan's raw frames, around the blurs known from the instrument (the thick sensor's depth kernel and the bandwidth as the renderer has them, the voxel's width), each spot with its own intensity, centre, tangential width, background and, on a deformed sample, radial spread. Also fitted to the spots below and above a 2theta, to check that it is the detector's (the same in pixels on every ring). `anri.calib.diagnostics.detector_profile` and `anri.calib.plots.plot_detector_profile` show the radial profile, data against both fits. The log prints the renderer's options (`--psf`, `--psf-wing`, `--sensor`). Checked by fitting spots the renderer made with a known response: recovered to ~0.01 px. `--thickness`: the source's length along the beam, a radial box (about thickness x tan(2theta)) convolved exactly, for a crystal measured in one rotation. `--convergence H V` (default 8e-5 rad, the Al CRLs'): the beam's angular spread, a radial width per spot from where a tilted beam diffracts (the crystal turned within its frame to satisfy Bragg).
- The renderer models the detector more fully, both optional and off by default. `geom["psf_mix"]` (`anri.io.imaged11.geom_from_pars(psf_mix=...)`): a point spread that is a mixture of Gaussians, ((fraction, sd), ...), in place of `sig_psf`; each component is rendered with its share of the intensity and the pixels summed before `min_value` applies. Measured on isolated spots, an Eiger2 CdTe at 43.6 keV is a sharp core (0.29 px) plus a 1.28 px wing holding a third of the counts, which one Gaussian cannot be. `geom["sensor"]` (`geom_from_pars(sensor=...)`): a thick sensor's depth kernel, (attenuation coefficient, thickness): each spot smeared outward along its ray by where its photons stop, a truncated exponential of scale sin(phi) / attenuation (phi the ray's angle to the sensor normal), rendered as 4 Gaussian slices (`anri.maths.utils.exponential_slices`); its mean shift is left to the calibration, which already holds it. Each costs one render per component or slice.
- `python -m anri.simulate prepare --psf-wing F SD --sensor LENGTH THICKNESS` (`stage_prepare(psf_wing=..., sensor=...)`): the detector's point-spread wing around `--psf`, and its sensor's absorption length and thickness (um), recorded in the prepare file and passed to the renderer. Both off by default; prepare files from before read as without them. The skill's simulate reference says how to choose them.

### Fixed

- Theory pages: the distortion that moves reciprocal-lattice vectors is the inverse transpose of the real-space one, g -> (I + beta)^-T g0 ~ (I - beta^T) g0 (`lim-reciprocal`, `conventions.rst`). The Fourier slice therefore sees the rows k and z of the real-space distortion, not its columns, and its three weak numbers per frequency are two dislocation components with Burgers vector along the ray and the shear d_k u_xi, which dislocation-free fields carry (`lim-nye`, `lim-weak-parts`, `lim-compatible`; 3D in `rf-3d`). The pages had said the weak part was the dislocation density with lines along z and that a compatible field had none; their proofs assumed beta = grad u for the reciprocal operator. The refiner's curl of its first moments is the Nye tensor less twice the curl of the strain (`rf-curl-reciprocal`), its split acts on a sample-frame operator M = Z (U0 B)^-1 rather than Z, and the no-dislocation output fills two weak numbers, not three. New proofs derive g from the deformed UBI and the Nye tensor by differentiation.
- Theory pages: filters are explicit. Truncating the singular values (`rf-project`), the no-dislocation constraint, an iteration stopped early (the solve; MLEM's fixed counts in the indexer) and the masks' filtered inputs are **Filter** paragraphs, never defaults, with the unfiltered result reported beside them; the index page states the rule and that proofs derive from definitions.
- Theory pages: bias along a weak direction grows as 1/sin theta for the first moments (1/sin^2 theta only for the metric's C_xixi), not 1/sin^2 theta; strain moves a peak by -2 eps tan theta for a lattice strain; the beam weight of `int-frame-weight` enters once (the per-peak amplitude holds none); `rf-weight` keeps the second-order mean of the rotation; the refiner's guarantee states that boxes from an indexer fitted to the same data, and the indexer's error bound, do not meet its conditions; omega's clipped arcsine is 1.6 frames at the axis edge.
- Theory pages: why the refiner needs regions, and what their data-dependence costs (`refiner.rst`). A moment refiner with no model of the sample's spread needs regions that hold all of their populations' light: otherwise two distributions with the same mass and mean put different light in a region (`rf-forced`). Fixed bins do not escape it (per-reflection bins are not common unknowns; cells of F have boxes as their complete regions, which corrects the histograms-and-moments section). The alternative, each voxel's distribution resolved finely enough to linearise the instrument's kernel (`rf-cell-linear`), needs ~1e5 cells for a 0.1 deg spread and ~1e8 for 1 deg per voxel at ID11's width, against 16 moments. A region chosen from the same data (the indexer's boxes) is biased by at most the light in the band between the boxes of the indexer's extreme placements, a fixed region measured from the data (`rf-sandwich`): no data splitting is needed.
- Theory pages: no coupling between voxels. The no-dislocation constraint and the truncated singular value decomposition are no longer options of the refiner (`limits.rst`, `refiner.rst`): every voxel is estimated by the left inverse with its own error bar and bias bound, and flagged where those exceed the precision sought; the merged-domain split is stated to be an invertible change of variables, coupling nothing. The indexer's remaining couplings are written out as filters (`localsearch.rst`): coarse levels bin space, inherited candidates restrict the last level's support (a feature needs at least `min_share` of a coarse voxel, `ls-support`), and MLEM keeps its start along directions the data do not determine (two equal columns keep their ratio, `ls-start`); with what would remove them.
- AGENTS.md holds no formulas: it points to the theory pages, with the hard rule that all code follows them (its omega-width formula had the sign of the rotation about the axis wrong). The skill gives strain's 2-theta shift its sign.

- `anri.simulate.diagnostics.probe_rows` (behind `plot_survival`) judges what each peak keeps by the expected recorded value the render stage's realisation has (Poisson counts, the cut and the group rule, `recorded_moments` on the row's summed light), not by rounding the light to the cut; it takes `pixels_in_spot` and `lost_share` (a peak is gone where it keeps less than this share, default 5 %).
- The renderer takes the beam's weight along the scan's path when dty moves during a rotation (fly scans): within each frame, dty moves from its reading (the frame's centre) at the rate the readings' central differences give (`make_row`'s new `dty_rate_sorted`), so the beam's fold and each frame's residual weight are evaluated where the voxel is when the peak's light arrives (theory page, intensity: `int-dty-motion`, `int-dty-rate`). Before, the fold used the row's middle dty and each frame its one reading. Rows whose dty is constant render exactly as before (rate exactly 0). A voxel crossing a 0.1 um sd beam while dty moves one sd per 0.1 deg frame: frames 8x coarser now agree with fine frames to 0.1-0.5% (the fold's error for a weight that is not log-quadratic), against 6-40% with each frame's one reading.
- The indexer's likelihood ratio sums each orientation's entries per bin before taking a bin's term: entries of one orientation that share a histogram bin were each charged as if alone (3.6% off on a test with coarse bins). Where the model is clipped at 1e-6 of a bin, its drop is the clipped model's.
- The indexer's censored bins are one objective: an empty bin is the observation that its count was at most the censoring level, a saturated bin's that it was at least its data, and the MLEM ratio (`censored_ratio`), the deviance (`deviance`) and the likelihood ratio all use that censored Poisson likelihood (the ratio is the count's conditional expectation, the EM step). Before, the update pulled an empty bin above the level towards half of it while the deviance charged 2 (mu - c), so SQUAREM's safeguard compared a different function from the one MLEM climbed. `anri.index.occupancy.censored_below` and `saturated_above` compute the terms through the regularised incomplete gammas, with exact or bounded series where they underflow, a Poisson log-probability without cancellation at large counts (Stirling's series, DLMF 5.11), and, for saturated bins below d + 10 sqrt(d), the lower gamma's convergent series summed to a proved bound (JAX's regularised gamma was 9% wrong in log P at d = 1e5 in float32; now 1e-6, the ratio to float precision). Censor 0 is plain Poisson MLEM as before. The theory page proves each piece.
- `anri.crystal.orientation.laue_rotations` removes duplicate operations exactly (on the integer matrices, before the Cartesian transform) instead of rounding its rotations to 8 decimals: they are orthogonal to float precision, not to 5e-9.
- The renderer's thick-sensor kernel (`geom["sensor"]`) runs under `shard_map` with JAX 0.4.30 (Python 3.9): its 2x2 solve is written out, as `jnp.linalg.solve` lowers to `custom_linear_solve`, which that version's `shard_map` has no replication rule for.
- The fit's beam profile (`--beam`) has no negative row weights: in float32 `chord_weight`'s four-term difference can cancel slightly below 0, which left 39 bins of a Renk_a model at down to -0.005 counts (MLEM assumes non-negative weights).
- The fit with a beam profile (`--beam`) spreads each level's voxels over the rows of that level's step: the coarse level used the fine rows' count (9 rows of 0.4 um for a 0.5 um beam on 0.2 um steps, where 7 reach as far), about 30% of its time.
- SQUAREM (`anri.index.occupancy.squarem`, both MLEMs of the indexer) no longer repeats refused steps: after a refusal the step length is capped at a quarter of the refused one, 4x larger after a step taken at the cap. Near convergence the uncapped length grew to 50-60, every step was refused (a third of each iteration wasted) and the next proposed as long a step again: on Renk_a of ihma936 (level 1, 373k voxels x 256 candidates) iterations 6-10 of 10 were all refused. Now the same 10 iterations reach a deviance 0.3% lower (2.1673e11 against 2.1737e11), 4.5x the progress over iterations 5-8, with second populations 2 voxels from a boundary 37 -> 32.5% and 3 voxels 17 -> 14%. Fits are unchanged until a step is refused. The refinement's row-summed fits converge further too: 7525 orientations kept against 7233.
- The fit's later levels (`anri.index.occupancy.inherit_candidates`) give a fine voxel the orientations of the coarse voxel holding it first, then its 8 neighbours'. All were ranked by their largest occupancy in any of the 9, so a neighbour's main grain pushed out the voxel's own minor orientations once the neighbourhood held more than `--cand` / 27 of them: estimated from a fine fit of Renk_a (ihma936), in 67% of voxels, losing 1% of their occupancy on average and over 5% in 5% of voxels. The stage logs the share of the holding voxel's occupancy that did not fit in `--cand` (raise it if large); `inherit_candidates` returns it per voxel. It also logs the share of the occupancy not subdivided (below `--min-share`, or beyond `--per-voxel`: a grain spread over many orientations, as in a deformed metal, loses its tails there, narrowing its spread; 0.02% on Renk_a).
- The renderer (`anri.fwd.render.render_row`, per-voxel windows) follows a voxel across the beam while it diffracts: the beam's weight, expanded to second order in omega about the peak's centroid (autodiff, any profile), is folded into the peak's (slow, fast, omega) Gaussian before the frame integration (`_beam_fold`); each frame keeps the residual of the true weight (its own dty, any profile) against that expansion. Before, each frame took the beam's weight at the peak's mean omega in it, wrong within the frame: frames 8x coarser held 0.7% different pixels at the beam's centre and 4.6% (totals 4% low) at 3 beam sd, for a voxel 50 um off the axis with a 0.03 deg spread; now 3e-4. Splat (one spot shape per grain) keeps per-frame weights. The fold's curvature is clamped to <= 0 and its shift to 3 sd (at omega = 0 and 180 deg a square voxel lines up with the beam: a kink in its weight, where point derivatives are meaningless and an unbounded fold rendered pixels of 3.6e9 counts); far outside the beam, where the weight underflows, there is no fold.
- float32 stays float32 under `jax_enable_x64`: `anri.maths.rotation.rot_x/y/z` keep a Python angle weakly typed (they built a float64 matrix from `rot_z(0.0)`), `anri.geom.gonio.sample_to_lab`/`lab_to_sample`/`origin_lab` and `anri.diffract.scatter.omega_solns_core` build their constant vectors in the input's dtype, and `anri.fwd.render._peak_cov` passes zero beam tilts of the lattice's dtype (a Python 0.0 became a float64 primal of the covariance propagation). A float32 centroid and covariance were silently computed in float64. Float64 results are unchanged.
- `anri.geom.beam.chord_weight` in float32: the switch from the trapezoid to the box form near omega = 0, 90, 180, 270 deg is at (a - b) < 3e-3 voxel sizes (1e-6 in float64, unchanged), where the trapezoid's four-term difference stops cancelling. Within ~0.01 deg of the kink, float32 weights were up to 30% off.
- The renderer in float32: the beam weight's floor before its log is the dtype's smallest number (1e-300 is 0 in float32, which gave -inf and infinite pixels), and cells of frames holding none of a peak's mass (0 / 0 in the truncated moments) are 0, not NaN.
- Population fractions (`python -m anri.index populations`, `anri.index.populations.group`) count every candidate of a voxel. Only the 16 most occupied were grouped, but each fraction was divided by the occupancy of all of them: a fit that spreads a grain over many children (a local search, 0.1 deg apart) under-reported the main population, and second populations. On DTL1z45 of ma4752, main fraction median 0.87 -> 0.94 and voxels with a second population 18 -> 25% (standard route). `m` (default now all) still limits the candidates; voxels per call scale with it (`vb`, default 2^18 / m).
- Back-projections (`anri.index.mask.reconstruct`, `anri.index.diagnostics.back_projection`) of a full turn join its two halves first (`anri.index.mask.stitch_turn`): the ray at omega + 180 is the one at omega mirrored about y0. Each half was ramp-filtered as it was, and a half-sample scan's projections are cut off sharply near the axis: the filter turned the cut into a spike, back-projected as a bright centre (2x on a uniform disc) and radial streaks.
- `back_projection` uses the histogram stage's new sinogram at the frames' omega step (`H_sino`). The fit's 1 degree bins are too few views for a filtered back-projection beyond ~ystep / 1 deg from the axis (14 um for 0.25 um steps): the image was streaked there. Histogram files from before fall back to the fit's bins.
- Coarse voxels (`python -m anri.index fit --coarse`, `anri.index.data.coarsen_scan`) model a partial last group of rows: when the rows are not a multiple of the group, the last coarse row sums fewer rows, and without an exposure array the model put a whole group's intensity there. That row is the scan's end: on a half-sample scan, the rows past the axis. It now gets a per-row exposure, the share of the group it holds; `anri.index.occupancy.system` takes an exposure of [n_rows, 1] as well as [n_rows, n_o].
- The histogram stage reads only the scans the DataSet holds (`anri.index.stages.open_dataset`), in its order. It read every group of the sparse file, so a DataSet of a subset of the rows (e.g. a half-sample scan without the rows past the axis) failed on the first group it did not hold. A scan the DataSet names but the sparse file lacks is an error.
- The voxel fit's default mask (`--mask disc`, `anri.index.mask.disc_mask`) covers a half-sample scan's whole sample. On a scan over a full turn it reaches the scan's farther end from y0: a voxel within it is in a scanned row for at least half the turn, every direction once. It took the nearer end, so a half-sample scan (dty from one edge of the sample to a few um past the axis) kept only those few um round the axis. Scans over half a turn are unchanged.
- Rendering a big grain map in many calls (`python -m anri.simulate render`, every row of a scan or every `--frames-per-call` chunk of a box beam's rotation) no longer redoes per-map host work on every call, which kept the GPU idle on a DCT-sized map (500 x 500 x 200 voxels: 20 min on an L40S). `write_rendered_rows` puts each phase on the device once, with the new `anri.fwd.render.to_device`, which also sorts a grain map's entries by grain; `render_row` used to copy the whole map to the device, copy its grain labels back and sort them on the host, on every call. Spots are numbered without sorting every (voxel, spot) pair on the host (`np.unique`), and splat's check that `sig_rot` is the same over a grain runs on the device. A short last `--frames-per-call` chunk is padded to the others' length, so it compiles nothing new. Pixels are unchanged.

- `ty check` passes again: `anri.diffract.factors.extinction` takes array-likes as it says, and `anri.fwd.render`'s entries dict is typed as one.
- A command remade from a stage file (`run_command`) gives a one-item list with its comma: `--rings 9,` is ring 9, where `--rings 9` meant the first 9 rings. `python -m anri.calib` skips the empty item in `--free` and `--tols`.
- The renderer's `min_value` (`anri.fwd.render.render_row`, `render_phases`) is now a cut on each pixel's summed value, after every peak (and phase) of the row is added. It cut each peak's own share of a pixel before, in the per-voxel path, so many weak voxels lighting one pixel lost their intensity: on a 27k-voxel grain map of unit densities, the default 1e-3 dropped 77% of it (splat, which sums a spot first, lost almost none). Every non-zero cell now reaches the host merge: on CPU that row took 7.9 s instead of 4.5 s, 2.0 GB peak memory instead of 1.7.
- `anri.index.mask.backproject` (and so `reconstruct` and `anri.index.diagnostics.back_projection`) failed with float64 enabled (`jax_enable_x64`): the beam direction came out float64 and its rows' weights no longer matched the float32 accumulator. Each row's contribution is now cast to the accumulator's dtype.
- Padded voxels no longer take intensity in the voxel fit. `anri.index.occupancy.pad_voxels` padded with voxels at (1e6, 1e6), which sit on the rotation axis's ray at omega = 135 and 315 degrees and so took part in candidate selection and MLEM there (without absorption). Padding is now marked by z = NaN and `system` gives it no entries. Reported in [#51](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/51).
- On a full-turn scan, a prediction within half an omega bin of the seam keeps the corner on the other side (`anri.index.data.eta_omega_corners` wraps omega when n_o b_o = 360, as it wraps eta). It lost up to half its weight there, in the filter and the voxel fit. Partial scans still drop the corners past their ends. Reported in [#51](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/51).
- Fly and helical scans: each (row, omega bin)'s dty offset and exposure (`anri.index.data.dty_offsets`) take the same frames as the histogram. Frames past the scan's ends were wrapped into the other end, and the histogram stage put frames in rows by each row's mean dty instead of the DataSet's dty that binned them. Reported in [#51](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/51).
- `anri.index.occupancy.censored_ratio` and `deviance` are jitted, so XLA fuses them instead of making several histogram-sized temporaries per MLEM iteration. Reported in [#51](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/51).

### Changed

- The renderer compiles in about two thirds of the time (XLA:CPU, the sharp-spot render test: 14.4 to 9-10 s, half of the program's equations): `_beam_fold` takes the beam's log weight, slope and curvature in one forward-over-forward pass instead of `value_and_grad` plus `grad(grad)`, and the beam profile (`anri.geom.beam._smoothed`, `chord_weight`, `beam_weight`) evaluates the points it sums as one stacked array, so each beam weight inlines 6 copies of `ndtr` instead of 24. Same arithmetic per element: Gaussian-beam renders are bitwise unchanged, others differ at rounding level (1e-15 off axis, 1e-9 for a flat-top beam's fold, whose curvature differences a cancelling sum).
- Simulated data are a realisation of the recording model (theory page "What the detector records"): `python -m anri.simulate render` (`stage_render`, `simulate_sparse`, `write_rendered_rows`) draws each pixel's count from Poisson(expected light) and records it as ImageD11's segmentation does, counts above `--cut` in 8-connected groups of at least `--pixels-in-spot` (default 3) pixels per frame. It used to round the expected light and keep every pixel above the cut, with no group rule. New options, recorded in provenance: `--pixels-in-spot`, `--noise poisson|round` (default `poisson`; `round` is for tests only) and `--seed` (default 0; row i draws with (seed, i), whichever rows a job renders). `run_command` remakes a render file made before this with `--noise round --pixels-in-spot 1`, as it was made
- `anri.io.imaged11.write_scan` writes the counts it is given (non-negative integers, else ValueError) and no longer takes `cut`; the segmentation is `anri.io.imaged11.segment` (the cut and the group rule, checked against ImageD11's `lima_segmenter`), the counts `anri.simulate.stages.record`
- `PIXELS_IN_SPOT` lives in `anri.io.imaged11` (ImageD11's segmentation default), used by the indexer and the simulation
- `anri.calib.detector.find_spots` labels blobs with `anri.io.imaged11.pixel_groups`, which no longer joins a pixel on the detector's last row to one on the first row of the next frame
- Populations (`python -m anri.index populations`) are grouped within `anri.index.populations.radius`: one bin of the fit's histogram (1 deg), or 1.8 x the fitted orientations' spacing if coarser (a plain fit of a grid). It was 1.8 x the search grid's step whatever the fit refined to: 4.5 deg for a 2.5 deg search, which merged any misorientation below that (cell walls in deformed metals) into one population with a large spread. Populations closer than a bin share bins in every reflection, so the data cannot separate them. The radius is logged and kept in `resolved` (`radius_deg`). `anri.index.plots.plot_populations` takes it as the top of its spread colours (was 2 x the grid step). On Renk_a of ihma936 (second populations 27-60 deg away: other grains, twins) the maps barely change: voxels with a second population 34.7 -> 34.8%.
- `--censor` (filter and fit stages, `anri.index.diagnostics.grid_deviances`) is measured by default, no longer 0: 3 pixels per spot (ImageD11's `pixels_in_spot` default, not in the sparse file) times the smallest recorded pixel, the cut + 1 monitor-normalised, which is the histogram's smallest non-empty bin since pixels are binned whole (`anri.index.occupancy.censor_level`). It is the least a spot can hold and go unrecorded, so an empty bin below it agrees with the model. The level used is logged and kept in each stage's `resolved` (`censor_used`); `--censor 0` gives plain Poisson as before. On Renk_a of ihma936 (cut 1): 3 x 1.89 counts. A fit's lit curve (the share of bins with data against the model) is no measure of it: there half the bins are lit only at 180-1000 counts, set by misfit, not by the cut.
- The fit stage (`python -m anri.index fit`, `anri.index.stages.stage_fit`) fits coarse to fine in orientation and space, and replaces fit + localsearch (filter -> fit -> populations). First it refines the filter's orientations on the row-summed data, without tomography, until their worst case is `--refine-to` (default 0.3 deg; `anri.index.local.refine`: 27 children a third of the step apart, kept by the filter's `--min-lr` and `--keep`). Then level 0 picks each voxel's `--cand` (default now 256) best among all of them on voxels about R x the worst case wide (R the mask's radius: an orientation error e moves a voxel's predicted row by up to R e; `anri.index.local.level_rows`), and each of `--levels` more (default 1) subdivides the orientations by 3 and the voxels down to the dty step: a voxel's orientations holding at least `--min-share` of it (default 0.01, at most `--per-voxel`, default 16) pass their 27 children to the finer voxels of its 3 x 3 neighbourhood, each started at its parent's occupancy / 27 (`anri.index.local.inherit`, `anri.index.occupancy.inherit_candidates`, which now also returns those starts and never offers an empty slot, `anri.index.occupancy.coarse_positions`). Grid orientations put spots in the wrong 1 deg bins: on a synthetic phantom the old route left a checkerboard in every grain (61% of the phantom's voxels occupied on a full scan, 36% on a half-sample scan, which also emptied the voxels near the axis and made streaks); the new fit occupied 100%, with twins 98% right (90% before). On DTL1z45 of ma4752 (half-sample scan, 556k voxels) its prototype removed the streaks through the axis, in 15 min against 46. A share rather than localsearch's 95% cover keeps a thin twin that is a small share of a voxel. `--refine-to 0 --levels 0` is the old plain fit of the grid's orientations, as `python -m anri.calib fit` needs: the calibration refuses a fit of refined orientations (resolved `orientation_step` below the search grid's). The fit records `orientation_step`, `worst_case_deg` and `rows_per_level`; `callback` is called at the last level. `run_command` remakes an old plain fit with `--refine-to 0 --levels 0`, and refuses a chain with a local search or `--coarse`.
- `python -m anri.index histogram --pol-factor` (`stage_histogram(pol_factor=...)`, default 1): the beam's degree of horizontal polarisation, in the later stages' predicted intensities, was fixed at 1. The indexer's geometry no longer carries a made-up bandwidth and divergence (2e-3 and 1.5e-4 rad): nothing in the indexer used them, only the renderer does. Histogram files from before read back with a polarisation of 1, as they were made.
- `anri.fwd.render.render_row` has one outline for both ways of rendering (select the peaks, give each a window size, render one size at a time, merge): the per-voxel windows (`_render_windows`) and splat (`_render_spots`) differ only in how a batch is rendered. Pixels and stats are unchanged (bit for bit, with and without splat, several window sizes and small batches).
- `simulate_sparse` and `write_rendered_rows` moved from `anri.io.imaged11` to `anri.simulate.stages`: the loop that renders a scan's rows belongs with the render stage, and `anri.io` no longer imports the renderer. `anri.io.imaged11.write_scan` still writes each row.
- `anri.index.occupancy.add_cells` and `take_cells`: the masked scatter-add and gather into histogram cells that both MLEMs (the voxel fit and the filter's orientation fit) each wrote out four times.
- One function turns a stage's recorded options back into its command line: `anri.io.provenance.command_line`, which reads the subcommand's argparse options (separate values, a repeated flag, comma-separated, or `--no-x` for an option on by default). `run_command` in `anri.index.stages`, `anri.calib.stages` and `anri.simulate.stages` each had its own copy of these rules. Each command line is built by a `parser()` function in its `__main__`, and `--iter` (and `anri.calib rings --mask`) now parse to the stage's own option names, `n_iter` (and `detector_mask`).
- The indexer's selection helpers and constants moved out of `anri.index.stages`, next to what they select, so that plots and diagnostics no longer import the stages: `select_by_likelihood` to `anri.index.orientations`; `select_occupied`, `select_populations`, `occupancy_weighted` and `N_POP` to `anri.index.populations`; `lit_map` to `anri.index.search`; `voxel_grid` to `anri.index.mask`; `sample_groups` and the histogram bins `B_E`, `B_O`, `R_E`, `R_O` to `anri.index.data`.
- An ImageD11 phase's cell keys (`cell__a` ... `cell_lattice_[P,A,B,C,I,F,R]`) are read and written in one place: `anri.io.imaged11.lattice_from_cell` and `cell_from_lattice`, used by the indexer, the calibration and the simulation (each had its own copy of the keys). The calibration's `rings` stage now says a centring letter needs a space-group number, as the indexer did.
- Figures that survive a restart of the Jupyter server: the Guides page says how (JupyterLab's *Save Widget State Automatically*, `"saveState": true`). ipympl then saves each figure as last drawn in the notebook, and after a restart it shows as a picture where "Error displaying widget: model not found" was; checked in JupyterLab 4.6 with ipympl 0.10 (it fixes ImageD11's notebooks too). Every guide notebook with widgets also has a static mode for the docs: `interactive = False` (now also in the calibration and indexing-parameters guides, as in the rendering and frame-comparison guides) draws each figure once at the parameters' values as an inline image, with no widgets. Drawing a mask by hand still needs `interactive = True`.
- The calibration guide takes every option of `python -m anri.calib rings`: a dense powder image instead of the scan's own pixels (`image`), and `ring_phase`, `detector_mask`, `spatial`, `scans`, `threshold`, `ring_iter`, `n_eta`, `ring_clip` and `merge`. Its last cell prints every command that remakes the calibration from the dataset (`anri.calib.stages.commands`).
- A theory page on calibrating the geometry (`docs/source/theory/calibration.rst`): the powder rings (each (ring, η sector)'s residual, the Levenberg-Marquardt fit in rounds, why tilt_x and the wavelength are left out, the centre and distance searches), the scan's peaks (matching along the beam with parallax, the residuals, the Schur complement over the orientations, what the scan separates that rings cannot, the rounds), and the strain left. The theory section now covers `anri.calib`.
- A calibration's provenance goes back to its dataset through the powder rings. `anri.io.imaged11.write_pars(..., source=...)` records in `pars.json` the stage file it was written from, with its id (`"anri_source"`; ImageD11 reads only `geometry` and `phases`), and `python -m anri.calib pars` (`anri.calib.stages.write_pars`) gives it. The indexer's histogram stage, given such a `pars.json`, records that file as its source if it is still there and unchanged (it logs why not otherwise), so `show` prints the rings, the indexing and the calibration in one chain. `anri.calib.stages.commands` returns every command that remakes a calibration or rings file from the dataset (`rings`, `pars`, the indexer's `run` with `--outdir`, `fit`), and `run_command` now also takes a rings file. `anri.index.stages.run_command` stops at the latest histogram stage, so a calibration before it adds none of its options. `python -m anri.calib rings --merge` (the ring merging distance) was missing from the command line.
- `python -m anri.calib fit` converges, and refuses a local search's map. Each round re-matches the peaks with the new geometry. After the listed tolerances, the last is now repeated while a round still moves a free parameter by more than its standard deviation (`--extra-rounds`, default 6). If it still has not converged it says so: its quoted errors are then too small. A local search's file is refused, naming its fit stage file. Its orientations, 0.11 deg apart, let each round's matching pick the one that absorbs part of the geometry's error, so the rounds close only ~20% of the gap each. On the Ni phantom, from a powder-ring start, distance and wavelength were still 1e-4 off after the default rounds, against 1e-6 from the fit stage's map. The full chain on that phantom (`anri.calib rings` from a start 7% off in distance, 1.8% in wavelength and 100 px in centre, then `anri.index`, then `anri.calib fit` on the fit map) ends within 6e-6 in distance and wavelength, 1e-4 px in centre and 1e-7 rad in the tilts. It is also faster: 54 s on a shared GPU for 802k peaks and 9 rounds, against 89 s for 4 rounds before. Matching is in float32 (8x faster on a GPU than float64; matches identical but for near-ties). Peaks are padded to one size for every round, so nothing recompiles between rounds. Empty map columns are dropped (10 of 64 used). `--row-step N` uses the peaks of every Nth dty row only, for CPU-only machines: 53 s on 16 CPU cores for the Ni phantom with `--row-step 10`, same geometry as all rows within 1e-6.
- `anri.utils.setup` turns on JAX's persistent compilation cache (`cache_dir`, default `JAX_COMPILATION_CACHE_DIR` or `~/.cache/anri/jax`; "" turns it off). A later run reuses what an earlier one compiled: `python -m anri.calib fit` on the Ni phantom takes 23 s instead of 40 s on a GPU the second time (compiling 1.4 s instead of 18 s). A changed function gets a new key and compiles again. The cache is capped at 2 GB only if the `filelock` package is installed (without it JAX turns the whole cache off when given a cap). The JAX used here (0.8.2) keeps only GPU compiles.
- Every iteration of a SQUAREM-accelerated MLEM fit (the voxel fit, each local-search level, the filter's orientation fit) is now logged, with its step length, whether the extrapolated step was taken, and the deviance; it was every fifth. The numbers were on the host already, so this adds no transfers.

- The docs' user guide follows rietx's split: Tutorials, which teach Anri in order, and a new Guides section, each page one task ("How to ..."): simulating a dataset (`rendering_parameters`), choosing the indexing parameters, comparing measured frames with a forward model, and exporting a powder pattern. The tutorials' geometry prose, which repeated the Theory section, is now a pointer to the Reference frames page. Five tutorials are rewritten to go with the Theory pages and check them: crystallography (the B matrix of a hexagonal cell, the UBI's rows, an extinction by atom positions, the Laue group), the goniometer (each rotation's sign, wedge and chi tilting the omega axis, ImageD11's wedge sign, dty and y0 giving each voxel's sinusoid), the detector (pixel to lab and back, the o-matrix, tilts, and FABLE's test case), a simple forward model (every centroid of a grain with `anri.fwd.peak.get_centroid_both`, which ImageD11 indexes back to the same grain) and the forward model with errors (position, wavelength and divergence widths, each checked against its formula, the vertical divergence's |cot eta| over every reflection of a grain).

- `python -m anri.index --mask auto` is now the sample's support in the projections (`anri.index.mask.support_mask`): the voxels whose row is lit at every omega the scan covers them (each omega's projection scaled to its bright rows and median-filtered along omega), largest region, convex hull. It is no longer cut to the disc, so a sample larger than the disc is fitted whole. The previous automatic mask, a threshold of a filtered back-projection of log intensities, picked out the bright edges of a strongly absorbing sample and lost its inside. On a large, coarse-grained Ag2S sample the support still rounds the corners (a row clipping a corner crosses little material, so few spots), so `disc` (with `--pad`) stays the default. The fit stage saves the support image as `support` (was `recon`).
- The theory pages follow the code again. Intensities: the renderer models absorption along each path, from the map itself or a given map of coefficients (it was described as a transmission per frame only). A new page, "Sharpening orientations off the grid", covers the local search (`anri.index.local`). Populations: the grouping radius is 1.8 steps of the search grid even after a local search, and `occupied` and `min_frac` choose what is reported. The voxel grid: `grid_pad` and `gridstep`. The rendering guide gains the absorption options (`cif`, `mu`, `mu_map`, `absorption`), `pol_factor`, `batch` and `peaks_table`, and is stored with a run at this version; the frame-comparison guide gains the instrument's options (`divergence`, `bandwidth`, `beam_fwhm`, `psf`, `pol_factor`) and absorption, and shows how its model was made. The powder guide lists its options.

### Added

- Grain maps for the renderer, e.g. from DCT: one UBI per grain and a grain label per voxel, so no orientation spread and one strain per grain. `anri.fwd.render.grain_entries` makes map entries from a label volume ([nx, ny, nz] cubes or [nx, ny] columns, centred on the rotation axis, sample axes) and the grains' UBIs: "grain" [N] and "grain_ubi" [G, 3, 3] in place of "ubi". `render_row` renders them as the same map with every voxel given its grain's UBI (the same peaks and pixels, tested), but finds the peaks that reach a row grain by grain first: a peak's omega does not depend on where its voxel is, so each (grain, hkl, branch) is tested against the row's omega range once, and only the voxels of those that pass are placed on the detector. On a DCT-like map (216k voxels, 10 grains, 78 hkls, 10 deg of frames, 4 CPU cores) selection takes 0.19 s instead of 2.5 s and the whole row 10.4 s instead of 13.4 s; the rest is integrating each peak over its window cells, which is per voxel either way. `render_row(..., splat=True)` renders a grain map spot by spot, a spot being a (grain, hkl, branch): every voxel's peak in it has the same omega and, to ~ voxel offset / detector distance, the same covariance, so each voxel's weight (density, beam, absorption, per frame) is shared bilinearly between the four pixels around its own (sc, fc) on a tile, and each frame of the tile is convolved once with one peak's window cells (`jax.lax.conv_general_dilated`, depthwise). Against the per-voxel render (tested): totals within 0.15%, pixels within 2% of the brightest, frame centroids within 0.01 px; on the map above, 1.4 s instead of 15.8 s for the row (11x), and 0.26 s instead of 6.0 s (23x) for 1 um voxels on 1 um pixels. Each device takes whole spots with their voxels, so nothing is merged across devices before the host. `sig_rot` must then be the same over each grain. The per-voxel cut `min_value` applies to the summed spot, not to each voxel's share, so faint voxels add up instead of being dropped one by one. `python -m anri.simulate prepare` reads a grain map from an `.npz` (with "grain" and "grain_ubi" instead of "ubi") and keeps its grains through render. Per-voxel maps, and everything that reads them (indexer, calibration), are unchanged.
- `python -m anri.simulate` simulates box-beam scans (DCT, 3DXRD) as well as scanning 3DXRD. `prepare --beam box` lights the whole sample with a flat-topped beam (`--beam-size H V`, default the sample plus two voxels; `--beam-fwhm` is then its edges' blur, default a voxel) in one rotation at y0, with cubes for a map of several layers; `--beam pencil` (the default) is unchanged. `render --splat` renders grain maps spot by spot, and `--frames-per-call N` renders each row N frames at a time (`anri.io.imaged11.write_rendered_rows`), so that a long rotation fits in memory; each call renders only its own frames, so the parts join without overlap. The DCT tutorial builds its map with `grain_entries` and renders with `splat`: 136 s for its 360° scan (512k voxels, 28M voxel peaks in 837 spots) on 4 CPU cores, against 784 s voxel by voxel; a new section renders 10° both ways (5 s against 60 s): totals within 0.15%, pixels within 1% of the brightest. Splat's kernels are computed once per window class, and voxels are added onto the tiles in fixed chunks, so a scan compiles a few times, not once per batch.
- Extinction, per phase, in the forward model (Becker and Coppens, 1974, after Zachariasen, 1967): `anri.diffract.factors.extinction_parameters` gives each reflection's primary and secondary extinction parameters from its absolute `|F|^2`, the cell volume, the wavelength and the phase's mosaic crystal (radius of a perfect domain, radius of the crystal, width of a Gaussian or Lorentzian mosaic distribution), with the absorption-weighted mean path through the crystal when an attenuation coefficient is given (first order, good to `mu R` about 1). `extinction` is their analytic fit (within 4% of their tables for sin(theta) <= 0.5), and `polarisation_extinction` extinguishes the two field components separately, as they do. `render_row` applies it when a phase's entries carry `"extinction": (x, X, gaussian)` (per reflection); without it nothing changes. Strong reflections of perfect crystals, such as a calibrant Si cube, come out weaker than kinematic `|F|^2`: on an undeformed Si cube at 65 keV the strong (h + k + l = 4n) reflections at low angle were 10-45% below their weak neighbours. The theory page on intensities has a section on it.
- The indexing guide (`docs/source/guides/indexing_parameters.ipynb`) uses the indexer's new options. Section 1 picks any rings from a list, not only the first (`rings`, `ring_merge`): the table lists every ring shown, used or not, with its multiplicity, `|F|^2` and gap. `anri.index.diagnostics.ring_windows` and `anri.index.plots.plot_rings` take a ring list as the histogram stage does, and measure each ring's width between its true neighbours. `ring_profile_cake` takes `merge`. Section 2 passes `detector_mask`, `dty_source` and `frame_dty`, and draws the detector coverage of each ring (`plot_coverage`). Section 9 sets `levels`, `cover` and `per_voxel` in the parameters, and has plots before and after the local search. Before: how many orientations each voxel needs to hold `cover` of its occupancy (`orientations_needed`, `plot_local_slots`, using `anri.index.local.covering`, now public), and the children a level predicts. After: the deviance at each level (`level_recorder`), and how far each voxel's main population moved, with its fraction and spread before and after (`local_change`, `plot_local`). The populations section draws the occupancy-weighted IPF colours (`ipf_z_occupancy`), and the last section points to each stage file's log.
- `anri.calib.diagnostics` and `anri.calib.plots` hold the scan fit's diagnostics, which were inline in the calibration guide, so that a workflow widget can bind them as the notebook does: `read_occupancy` and `occupancy_kept` with `plot_min_occ` (`--min-occ`; the stage's own cut, `anri.calib.stages.select_occupancy`), `match_distances` with `plot_match` (`--tols`, and again at the calibrated geometry), and for the result `read_calib`, `residual_trends` (`plot_residuals`, `plot_residual_hists`) and `strain_left` (`plot_strain_left`). `anri.calib.fit.split_strain` splits the strain left into hydrostatic and deviatoric parts and their peak-weighted means, for both the stage's log and the plot. The guide calls them, with the same numbers as before.
- The calibration guide (`docs/source/guides/calibration.ipynb`) runs the whole chain from a first guess far off. It runs `python -m anri.calib rings` on the scan's own sparse pixels, with plots of the centre search, the distance search and each ring's offset per η sector before and after the fit (`anri.calib.plots`, from `anri.calib.diagnostics.read_rings`). It then indexes with the rings' geometry (fit stage, no local search) and runs `python -m anri.calib fit`. `rings.find_centre` and `find_distance` also return their curves, which `stage_rings` stores in the file's `search` group. The phantom is now the 316L phantom's grains without their cells and twins: from the distance 7% short, the wavelength 1.8% short and the centre 60 and 90 px off, the chain ends 5e-6 from the true distance and wavelength (5 rounds), the centre within 0.01 px. The guide and `stage_fit` state the scope: calibrants and little-deformed samples. On the 316L phantom's cells (a few tenths of a degree) the rounds converge slowly, still 6e-4 out after ten, and the stage warns. A deformed sample's geometry is refined with its strains.
- The forward model models absorption along the incoming and diffracted paths, as the indexer does, with the same paths (`anri.geom.paths`). `anri.fwd.render.add_absorption` gives each map entry its optical depth to the sample's edge in every direction in the slice; `render_row` then scales each window frame by exp(-(depth in + depth out)) at that frame's omega. The sample is built from the map itself (`anri.geom.paths.attenuation_map`): a voxel holding entries is full, with its phases' attenuation coefficients weighted by their densities, so a multiphase map absorbs phase by phase; one slice per layer of a 3D map. Or it is given as a map of coefficients, e.g. from an absorption tomography (pores and unindexed phases are then included). `python -m anri.simulate prepare` models it by default when every phase has a CIF (`anri.crystal.structure.attenuation`) or `--mu` (one value, or NAME=VALUE per phase), or with `--mu-map` (a `.npy`); `--no-absorption` turns it off. `edge_distances` now also takes a map of coefficients, giving optical depths.
- `python -m anri.calib rings` (`anri.calib.stages.stage_rings`, `anri.calib.rings`, `<...>_rings.h5`): the first calibration step, as ImageD11's powder calibration. It fits the distance, beam centre, tilt_y and tilt_z, from the motors' values, to a known phase's powder rings, close enough for the sample to index. tilt_x moves no ring and the wavelength is degenerate with the distance, so both are left for `anri.calib fit`. The data are a dense powder image, or a DataSet's sparse pixels summed over every frame (`--scans` for fewer), with 2theta seen from the lab origin, as ImageD11 computes it. Each lit pixel is assigned to the nearest ring within a tolerance, re-assigned at every iteration so the window follows the ring. Each (ring, 1 deg eta sector) gives one residual, the intensity-weighted mean 2theta of its pixels minus the ring's, as each sector's peak does in ImageD11. The fit is Levenberg-Marquardt in rounds of tolerance (`--tols 0.2,0.1`; narrower windows, under a few pixels, alias). Rings closer than twice the widest tolerance to a neighbour are left out, since overlapping profiles pull each other's means. Since the motors' centre can be 100 px off, more than a ring gap, a search without ring assignment comes first (`--no-search` skips it). The centre is where the histogram of the pixels' radii is sharpest (`rings.find_centre`, grids of 8, 2 and 0.5 px up to `--max-shift` 200 px). The distance is the one whose ring windows catch the most intensity above a flat background (`rings.find_distance`, `--span 0.7 1.4` x the start). On ID11's CeO2 calibration image, from the motors (distance 240 mm, centre 38 and 102 px off), it lands where it does from a close start, 3 s in all: distance +11 um, centre 0.01 px and tilts 6e-5 from ImageD11's powder notebook result (+3 um, 0.01 px and 2e-5 with `--tols 0.1,0.05`). An undeformed Si crystal's sparse pixels (one crystal, 18k lit pixels) also work, from a centre 108 px and a distance 7% off. The file holds each sector's offset from its ring before and after (the straightened rings), and `pars` writes it as a `pars.json` to index with. On the Ni phantom's sparse pixels (207 scans, 8 s on CPU), from a distance 1% off, the centre 3 px and the tilts 2 mrad off, it returns to the truth within 2 sigma: 0.6 um (distance), 0.004 px (centre) and 2e-5 rad (tilts). With the wavelength also 1.8% off and kept, the distance absorbs it (+2%) and every ring falls within 0.014 deg of its prediction.
- An agent skill for running anri (`anri/skill/anri`, in the open Agent Skills format, shipped in the package; issue #48): `SKILL.md` gives the order of work, the questions to ask first (optics, energy, beam width, segmentation cut, monitor), how to run on a shared machine, which stage's output chooses each option, what to check in a stage file before believing it (its log, the row ratios, convergence, candidates, completeness) and what a map can claim. Reference files cover the indexer stage by stage (each option, the number that chooses it and the diagnostic or plot that computes it, and what each file holds), simulating and the forward model, calibrating the geometry (`python -m anri.calib`: rings, indexing, then the scan fit; what to ask first, the plot or number behind each option, and the residual and strain-left checks before believing a calibration), making a phantom to test a method (`anri.phantom`: which options give which microstructure, what it cannot make, the checks before using it, and how to judge a method on it), and the physics behind the rules. `tests/unit/test_skill.py` checks that every command, option, function and link it names exists, so a rename fails CI. The README says how to link it where an agent looks for skills.
- Each `anri.index`, `anri.simulate` and `anri.calib` stage file keeps its log: the dataset `provenance/log` holds every message the stage logged, timed from its start, whichever front-end ran it (command line, notebook, or a workflow system). A stage wraps the `log` it is given with `anri.io.provenance.recorder` and stores the messages with `write_log`; `read_provenance` returns them as `"log"` (empty for older files), and `show --log` (`python -m anri.index`, `python -m anri.simulate` and `python -m anri.calib`; `show(path, log=True)`) prints them for every stage of the chain.
- A calibration guide (`docs/source/guides/calibration.ipynb`, #47): runs `python -m anri.calib fit` from Jupyter, with a plot for each choice (the map's occupancy cut, the matching tolerance against the distance of each peak's (h, k, l) from integers) and for the result (residuals against omega, detector azimuth and dty; the strain left per orientation), then writes ImageD11 parameter files and prints the command line. With no map given it renders the 316L phantom with a wrong geometry, segments and indexes it. `anri.calib.stages.load_inputs` reads what the stage starts from (used by `stage_fit` too), and `anri.calib.stages.run_command` gives the command that remakes a calibration file.
- `python -m anri.index localsearch` (`anri.index.stages.stage_localsearch`, `<...>_localsearch.h5`): a stage between fit and populations that sharpens the fit's orientations off the search grid, around those each voxel uses (`anri.index.local`). Each level replaces every orientation a voxel uses (its most occupied, holding `--cover` of its occupancy, at most `--per-voxel`) by the 27 rotations of a 3 x 3 x 3 grid around it, a third of the step apart (`local.children`), and fits each voxel again over the children of its own orientations only (`local.inherit`, `fit_occupancy(given=...)`), with the fit's options. `run` includes it with `--levels 2` (a 1 deg grid to 0.11 deg; `--levels 0` skips it), and the populations stage takes either file. On the search grid alone, a twin whose grid point lands nearer the exact twin than the grain's own grid point lands to the grain explains the reflections the two share, and takes occupancy where there is no twin: on an undeformed Si cube a Sigma3 ghost held 30% of the occupancy and the crystal 15% (3 deg grid); with the local search the crystal holds 100% and the ghost 0%. On the def316l phantom (1 deg grid): main population median error 0.49 -> 0.19 deg, Sigma3 ghost occupancy 8% -> 4%, twin-lamella voxels wrong 51% -> 20% (interior 49% -> 12%), deviance -6%, 35 s on a GPU. On an Ag2S scan the lamellae sharpen (a lamella grain wins 49% -> 75% of the voxels ImageD11's back-projections give it, 9 min on a GPU for 92k voxels). Each voxel's candidates are its own orientations' children: a ranking over every orientation, as the fit does, lets a grain's many children crowd out a thin twin's. `anri.index.search.predictions` predicts long lists in chunks of one shape (`chunk`), for a local search's millions of orientations.
- `python -m anri.calib`: calibrate the detector and beam geometry on a scanning-3DXRD scan of an indexed sample, as ImageD11's silicon notebook does for a single rotation, but on a whole scan and any number of grains (`anri.calib.stages.stage_fit`, `<...>_calib.h5`; `pars` writes ImageD11 parameter files, `show` the provenance). Peaks are ImageD11's peaks table merged per 4D label and dty row (`anri.io.imaged11.read_peaks`; `read_dataset` returns the DataSet's `pksfile`). Each is matched to an orientation of an `anri.index` fit and a reflection, and scatters from where the beam crosses that orientation's voxels at its dty and omega (`anri.calib.fit.match`): the parallax a single-grain fit cannot model on a scan. The distance, beam centre, three tilts and the wavelength (a calibrant) or the lattice scale (any other sample; the two are degenerate) are fitted by Levenberg-Marquardt to the peaks' (sc, fc, omega), with each orientation's rotation eliminated by a Schur complement (`fit_geometry`, `calibrate`); wedge, chi and y0 stay fixed. `strain_left` then frees each orientation's lattice with the geometry fixed, the strain left measuring the calibration. On the Ni phantom, from a wavelength 1.8% off, a distance 1% off, the centre 3 px and the tilts 2 mrad off, it returns to the truth within 5e-6 (distance and wavelength) and 2 sigma (centre, tilts). On an undeformed Si cube (S3DXRD, 140 um crystal 290 um off the axis), from the wavelength 1.78% off, it leaves 0.2e-5 hydrostatic and at most 2e-5 deviatoric strain, where the geometry from ImageD11's notebook loop leaves -96e-5 and 29e-5 (wavelength 0.18982 against 0.18927 A, distance 150.77 against 151.10 mm).
- The theory section's Reference frames page (`docs/source/theory/frames.rst`): Miller indices, the crystal Cartesian frame (B), the sample frame (U), the lab frame and the goniometer stack, the angles of a diffracted ray and the detector frame (o-matrix, tilts, beam centre), each with its equation, the function that computes it and a diagram, and where Anri differs from the FABLE geometry it follows. Anri's equations reproduce FABLE's own test case (omega, theta, eta and the pixel). The diagrams are SVGs drawn by `docs/source/theory/figures/make_figures.py` (standard library only) as projections of the actual vectors.
- A Theory section in the docs (`docs/source/theory`): the physics and statistics of the forward model and the indexer, in order (conventions, the crystal, where a reflection diffracts, intensities, peak shapes, indexing as tomography, the orientation search, fitting occupancies, populations), each equation with the paper it comes from and the function that computes it, Anri's own choices marked as such, and what each part leaves out. `tests/unit/test_theory_docs.py` imports every function the pages name, so a rename fails CI instead of leaving a dead link. Citations, there and in docstrings, follow one style, written down in `CONTRIBUTING.md` ("Citing papers"). The orientation grids (`anri.crystal.orientation`) and `anri.maths.rotation.mat_to_quat` and `mean_rotation` cite the papers their methods come from (Roşca et al. 2014, Singh and De Graef 2016, Rowenhorst et al. 2015, orix, Shepperd 1978, Moakher 2002), each checked against the paper. So do the methods named in other docstrings: MLEM (Shepp and Vardi 1982, Lange and Carson 1984), the likelihood ratio (Wilks 1938, Chernoff 1954), Lorentz and polarisation (Kabsch 1988, Kahn et al. 1982), the mask's ramp filter and Otsu threshold, Rodrigues' formula, XCOM and Dans Diffraction.
- `python -m anri.index powder`: exports a dataset's sparse pixels, summed over every frame, as a powder pattern (`anri.index.stages.stage_powder`; an export, not a stage of the indexer). Pixels are read as the indexer reads them and binned by their centre in (2theta, eta); each bin is divided by the solid angle of the measured pixels in it (`anri.index.data.detector_cake`, with `pixel_solid_angle`; the detector mask removes gaps), giving the intensity per sr per frame. `anri.index.data.powder_pattern` gives its esd from the spread over eta (grain statistics: on an Ag2S scan 47 x the counting esd), each ring's measured share of its solid angle (`coverage`), and the share of measured pixel-frames holding a data pixel (`lit_fraction`), which marks the censored valleys between peaks where nearly every pixel was below the segmentation cut. Writes `<sample>_<dataset>_powder.h5` (with the cake and provenance) and a `.xye` (`write_xye`). `pixel_cake(counts=True)` also counts the pixels per bin. The new guide "How to export a powder pattern" says how to refine a structure against it with rietx (not a dependency): on Ag2S (72 keV) the refined cell matched the CIF's within 0.05%.
- `python -m anri.index` models the detector mask: the histogram stage reads the DataSet's segmenter mask (`--detector-mask` to give another; ImageD11's convention, non-zero for a masked pixel, `anri.io.imaged11.read_mask`; `read_dataset` returns its `maskfile`) and records the measured share of each (ring, eta bin) (`anri.index.data.detector_coverage`: the detector's pixels sampled 4 x 4 after distortion, within each ring's tolerance; also 0 where a ring leaves the detector), as `coverage` in the rings group. The search's completeness leaves out predictions on less than half-measured bins, and the filter's and the voxel fit's system matrices scale each prediction by its bin's coverage, so a reflection on a module gap is no longer taken as a missing grain. On an Ag2S scan (Eiger, 72 keV) the horizontal module gaps hid every ring at eta +-(65-86) deg, about 10% of the predictions, which the fit had read as zeros. Histogram files from before carry no coverage, and are fitted as before.
- One voxel grid for the whole pipeline: the histogram stage fixes it (`n` x `n` voxels of the row step, centred on the rotation axis, sized from the DataSet's y0) and writes it as a `grid` group, which `load_histogram` returns and the fit and populations stages use; a y0 chosen later moves no voxel. `--grid-pad` (histogram stage, sample units) widens it on every side, for a sample reaching past the scanned range, whose outer part would otherwise pile its intensity onto the grid's edge (on an Ag2S sample 50 um past the 417-voxel grid: the edge pile-up gone and the deviance 1.3% lower). `anri.geom.scan.regrid` moves an array between two grids centred on the axis (the same voxels, cropped or padded alike on every side). Masks carry their voxel size (`anri.index.mask.save_mask`, an .npz) and `load_mask` centres one onto the grid; a bare .npy, e.g. an ImageD11 mask, is taken to have voxels of the grid's size. The populations stage's TensorMap holds each voxel's sample-frame position (`sx`, `sy`, and `sz` = 0 for a slice).
- `python -m anri.index --saturation 65535` (histogram stage): pixels whose raw counts reached the detector's saturation (`anri.io.imaged11.stream_sparse(saturation=...)` flags them before any normalisation) make their bins lower bounds in the fit: where the model predicts at least the data there it agrees, below it the data pull it up (`anri.index.occupancy.censored_ratio` and `deviance` take the saturated bins' indices; the histogram stage stores them as `H_saturated`). Clipped peaks otherwise pull the occupancies, and absorption, down where spots are brightest.
- `python -m anri.index` models absorption whenever the histogram stage had a `--cif` (or `--mu` is given; `--no-absorption` to turn it off): each system entry is scaled by the transmission along the incoming and the diffracted path of that voxel and prediction through the sample, whose shape is the fit's mask (`anri.index.occupancy.system` takes positions followed by each voxel's edge distances; `fit_occupancy(absorb=(sample_map, step))` builds them, also for the coarse voxels of `--coarse`). The occupancies are then free of absorption, and the fitted model has it. A disc is the wrong shape unless the sample fills it: give the sample's mask. The fit stage records mu in its provenance. The filter stage's global orientation fit has no voxel positions and stays without absorption.
- Absorption, the parts below the methods: `anri.crystal.structure.attenuation(structure, energy_kev)` gives the linear attenuation coefficient (1/um) from a structure's composition and density (Dans_Diffraction's tables, which reproduce NIST XCOM); `anri.geom.paths` gives the path through the sample from each voxel to its edge. `edge_distances` builds a table `D[voxel, phi]` over the in-plane directions (the sample a prism of a 2D map; for each direction, the map resampled along it and summed backwards: 0.5 s on a GPU for 174k voxels x 360 directions), `ray_directions` the incoming and diffracted directions in the sample frame, `path_lengths` the path along each (`D / cos(elevation)`), and `transmission` exp(-mu (L_in + L_out)). Ag2S at 72 keV (mu = 2.2 /mm) transmits only 10-30% through the middle of a 0.8 mm sample.
- `python -m anri.index --pad` grows the disc mask (sample units): a sample a little larger than the disc the beam sees at every omega is fitted whole, instead of its outer part's intensity piling onto the disc's edge (seen as smeared occupancy where the disc clips a corner of the sample). A voxel at radius r is in the beam over (2/pi) asin(r_disc / r) of the rotation, 2/3 at 1.15 r_disc.
- The populations stage's TensorMap also holds `ipf_x_occupancy`, `ipf_y_occupancy` and `ipf_z_occupancy`: the main population's IPF colours dimmed by the voxel's occupancy (full brightness at the 99th percentile of the occupied voxels; `anri.index.stages.occupancy_weighted`), as ImageD11 weights its IPF maps by intensity. Thin lamellae and weakly diffracting regions show in them.
- `python -m anri.index --rings 9,10,11,17,20`: which rings to use, as indices from the lowest 2theta, as well as a number of first rings (unchanged). For low symmetry, where the first rings are weak, sit on low-angle scattering or overlap, pick strong rings well apart from their neighbours. `--ring-merge` (d*, default 1e-4 as before): reflections closer than this are one ring, so rings the data cannot separate are merged instead of split between two. `anri.index.data.ring_table` takes the indices and `merge`, and also returns `index`, and `tth_all`/`ds_all` of every ring up to the last one used; the histogram stage measures each ring's width between its true neighbours (all rings), so a window never reaches into an unused ring. A number of rings gives the same table as before.
- A CIF of fcc iron, `tests/data/cif/Fe_fcc.cif` (austenite, e.g. 316L; `Fe.cif` is bcc), for `--cif`. Without one, |F|^2 = 1: on AP1_1 the fit's intensity per ring was 0.55-2.7 x the data's, and 0.94-1.16 x with this CIF.
- Fly and helical scans, where dty moves while omega turns: `anri.io.read_frame_dty(ds)` gives each frame's dty, the DataSet's (corrected) dty of its row plus the motion within the row from the sparse file's raw readings (a straight line over the row's frames). `anri.index.dty_offsets` turns it into each row's offset from its nominal dty per omega bin, and `anri.index.system` (`scan["ddty"]`) and `backproject` put each voxel in the rows that really saw it. `python -m anri.index` does this by default when dty moves (`--no-frame-dty` to ignore it); step scans are unchanged. On a helical scan (one dty step per turn) the rows' frames are up to half a step off the row's mean dty. `--dty-source frames` bins each frame by its own dty instead of its row's: a helical rotation then spreads over 2-3 rows, some (row, omega) bins get no frames and some two rotations' worth (which made the maps' rings and holes), so `dty_offsets(return_exposure=True)` also gives each bin's exposure, which `system` (`scan["exposure"]`) and `reconstruct` scale by. On W2, holes inside the sample: 391 with the DataSet's rows and the offsets (the default), 813 binned by each frame's dty, 577 with the exposure.
- `anri.index.mlem` is SQUAREM-accelerated MLEM (Varadhan & Roland 2008): each iteration is three MLEM updates and an extrapolation, kept above 1e-3 of the plain update (MLEM multiplies, so an occupancy at 0 could never come back) and never fitting worse than plain MLEM. `--iter` now counts these iterations, default 10 (was 50 plain): on the def316l phantom 10 iterations fit twin lamellae better than 50 plain ones did (48% of their voxels wrong, against 52%), in two-thirds of the time, with the untwinned voxels' mean orientation 0.02 deg further from the truth (a converged fit puts a grain's occupancy on fewer grid points); 5 iterations match the old default in 40% of the time. `mlem(callback=...)` reports each iteration (the notebook records its plots with it). It holds at most four copies of the occupancies.
- `python -m anri.index --beam FWHM`: each voxel is spread over the dty rows by the beam's profile across dty (a Gaussian of that FWHM, integrated over the voxel at each omega, as `anri.fwd.beam_weight`), over as many rows as it reaches (`anri.index.beam_rows`), instead of linearly over 2 rows. Default 0: the 2-row model, as before. `anri.index.system` takes `n_beam` (the fifth element of `dims`) and the beam in `scan` (`sig_beam`, `width_beam`, `voxel`); `block_voxels` takes the entries per prediction. With FWHM = step the two models are close: on the def316l phantom and on a real AP1_1 scan (scored by how well the maps explain the pixels) the maps are as good either way, and the profile costs ~2.4x in the candidate pass. It is for beams wider than the step (overfocusing), where a voxel reaches more rows.
- `python -m anri.index --censor C`: ImageD11 keeps only pixels above its segmentation cut, so an empty histogram bin had every pixel below the cut. Where the model predicts fewer than C counts in an empty bin, it counts as agreeing with the model, in both MLEMs (`anri.index.censored_ratio`) and in the deviance and the pruning's likelihood ratio (`anri.index.deviance`). Default 0: empty bins are zeros, as before. `orientation_mlem` and `fit_occupancy` take `censor`; `orientation_mlem(return_model=True)` also returns the fitted histogram.

A drawn mask: `anri.index.draw_mask(image)` (a polygon on the image, as ImageD11's `InteractiveMask`), saved as a `.npy` of booleans in reconstruction order. `--occupied` then works within the mask, and the results hold `mask` (and `recon` with `auto`).

- `anri.index.orientation_mlem`: one occupancy per orientation, fitted by MLEM to the histogram with its dty rows summed, and each orientation's likelihood ratio (how much the fit worsens without it). `python -m anri.index` now prunes this way by default (`--prune likelihood`): the grid orientations above chance completeness are fitted, and those with a likelihood ratio above `--min-lr` (25) are kept. Unlike completeness, this uses the intensities and explains crowded spots jointly. On a crowded phantom with many small grains, 4k orientations kept this way recall 99.6% of the grains (90% of those under 5 µm²), where completeness needs 19k for 98.8% (70%). `--prune completeness` keeps the old behaviour. With likelihood pruning, `--min-comp` raises the completeness needed (default: the chance level). - `anri.index`: index scanning-3DXRD data from scratch, giving orientation populations per voxel. The sparse pixels are binned once into a coarse histogram and a row-summed lit map. An orientation grid over the fundamental zone is pruned by completeness, with each prediction's tolerance computed from the grid spacing, the measured ring widths and the frame step (`match_tolerances`). Then the kept orientations' occupancies are fitted per voxel by MLEM, all voxels jointly. Occupancies are sparse (candidates per voxel, from the first MLEM update) and the work runs in blocks of voxels, so memory does not grow with voxels times orientations. Optionally, candidates come from a coarse fit (`fit_occupancy(coarse=...)`). Each voxel's occupancy is grouped into populations with a fraction, a mean orientation, a spread and a completeness (`populations`). `python -m anri.index <analysisroot> <sample> <dataset>` runs it on an ImageD11 dataset and writes a TensorMap, the populations, and the populations as map entries for the renderer. Tutorials: `docs/source/tutorials/phantom.ipynb` and `indexing.ipynb` (stored runs).
- `anri.phantom`: 2D phantom microstructures (`polycrystal`): Voronoi grains, cells misoriented a little from their grain, and twin lamellae, on a reconstruction grid. A 316L-like phantom is in `tests/data/phantoms/am316l`.

The last covers one fundamental zone: a Rodrigues grid for cubic groups (about half the orientations of a cubochoric grid for the same worst-case spacing), and otherwise the cubochoric grid reduced to the zone plus a thin shell around it, so the zone's boundary has no gaps. Also `allowed_hkls` (systematic absences).
- `anri.io`: `read_par`, `read_dataset` (an ImageD11 DataSet's scan, without ImageD11), `read_pars_json`, `stream_sparse` (sparse pixels a chunk at a time, with each frame's dty row) and `tensormap_from_recon` (an ImageD11 TensorMap from maps in reconstruction order).
- `anri.geom`: `recon_positions` (the sample positions of a reconstruction grid) and `sino_shift_and_pad` (ImageD11's padding of a reconstruction).
- Optional per-entry orientation spread `"sig_rot"` in the renderer's entries (`anri.fwd.render_row`, `render_peaks`): the standard deviation, in radians, of each component of a small isotropic sample-frame rotation of the entry's lattice. It widens the entry's peaks in omega and on the detector through the same linearised propagation as the beam's energy spread and divergence (fine for spreads up to a few degrees), and window size classes (`max_frames`) account for it. Entries without it render exactly as before, at the same cost.
- `anri.fwd.render_row(max_frames=...)` (and `anri.io.simulate_sparse`): peaks broad in omega get windows with more frames, in a few size classes (window[0], 2 window[0] + 1, ... up to max_frames), so they are not clipped. `stats["window_frames"]` gives each peak's window.
- Optional `sig_omega` in the geometry (and `anri.io.geom_from_pars(..., sig_omega=...)`): an extra spread of every peak in omega, in degrees.
- A DCT tutorial (`docs/source/tutorials/dct.ipynb`): a full 360 degree scan of an 80^3 voxel polycrystal with a box beam and a near-field detector. It is stored with its outputs and not re-executed when the docs are built.
- `anri.geom.beam_basis`: unit vectors along a beam and across it (horizontal and vertical).
- `anri.io.beam_from_pars` and `geom_from_pars` take a beam direction, `k_in_lab` (default lab x).
- `anri.fwd.guess_batch_size`: the largest `batch` for `render_row` that fits in a fraction (default 25%) of the free GPU or host memory, from XLA's memory analysis of the compiled render step. Adds `psutil` as a dependency. Raises `RuntimeError` where XLA gives no usable memory analysis (e.g. jaxlib 0.4.28 on macOS).
- `anri.fwd.check_render`: checks rendered peaks of your own map and geometry against a Monte Carlo simulation of the beam spreads through the forward model, and reports the worst cell error and window capture for each peak.
- The renderer is public: `anri.fwd.render_row`, `make_row`, `select_peaks`, `render_peaks` and `beam_weight` (previously only importable from `anri.fwd._impl.render`).

### Changed

- The global orientation fit of `python -m anri.index filter` (`anri.index.orientations.fit`) is SQUAREM-accelerated MLEM, as the voxel fit: `n_iter` counts SQUAREM iterations (3 MLEM updates each), default 20 (was 20 plain updates). Under plain MLEM, decoys that borrow intensity from real grains faded slowly: on AP1_1, 20 updates left likelihood ratios down to -5e5 (removing those orientations would have improved the fit), 20 SQUAREM iterations -6e3, in 24 s against 12 s. With `--cand 256` after it, the voxel fit's deviance is 1% lower and 58-63 voxels inside the sample are empty instead of 67. SQUAREM's step length depends on the GPU's summation order, so runs on the same input differ by 20-50 of ~24,000 orientations at a ratio of 1 (up to ~70 in ratios below 100). Both fits run one SQUAREM loop, `anri.index.occupancy.squarem(step, f, n_iter)`, each with its own MLEM update.
- The beam profile is in `anri.geom.beam`, beside `beam_basis` and `beam_offsets`: `beam_weight` (was `anri.fwd.render.beam_weight`) and `chord_weight`, public now, which the indexer's system uses instead of a private function of the renderer.
- The Gaussian interval maths the renderer integrates peaks with is in `anri.maths.utils`: `bin_fractions`, `truncated_moments`, and `ramp_integral` and `ramp_integral2` (the antiderivatives of the Gaussian CDF, public now).
- `anri.render` is `anri.simulate` (`python -m anri.simulate prepare|render|assemble|run|show`): it simulates a dataset, and `anri.fwd.render` is the renderer it uses. Stage files and their names are unchanged; files made before record `python -m anri.render` in their command.
- Modules renamed for what they hold: `anri.fwd.base` is `anri.fwd.peak`, `anri.crystal.utils` is `anri.crystal.lattice`, `anri.geom.utils` is `anri.geom.beam` and `anri.index.predict` is `anri.index.search`. `anri.index.populations.populations` is `anri.index.populations.group` and `anri.index.orientations.orientation_mlem` is `anri.index.orientations.fit`.
- No more `_impl` folders: each package's modules are its public API, used by their full path, and a package's `__init__` only imports its modules (e.g. `anri.geom.sample_to_lab` is now `anri.geom.gonio.sample_to_lab`, `anri.index.stage_fit` is `anri.index.stages.stage_fit`, `anri.fwd.render_row` is `anri.fwd.render.render_row`). `anri.utils` and `anri.phantom` are single modules, so `anri.utils.setup()` and `anri.phantom.voronoi` are unchanged. The API reference lists the packages, each package page its modules, and each module page its functions. Module-level names without an underscore are now public too: `anri.fwd.base.make_propagator`, `anri.fwd.render.window_origins`, `anri.index.data.eta_bin`, `omega_bin`, `eta_omega_corners` and `scan_beam`, and `anri.index.stages.STAGES` and `N_POP`.
- `python -m anri.index` runs in five stages, each a subcommand writing one HDF5 file that the next reads: `histogram` (the binned data, measured ring widths and resolved scan), `search` (completeness of every grid orientation), `filter` (the orientations kept, by likelihood or completeness; with likelihood, every orientation fitted and its ratio, so `anri.index.select_by_likelihood` can redo the choice), `fit` (occupancies per voxel; `--y0` and `--mask` are options here, as they are read off the histogram) and `populations` (`--occupied`, `--min-frac`; `_entries.npz` and `_tmap.h5`); `run` does all five, and `show <file>` prints how any stage file was made, back to the dataset. A stage can be re-run with other options and `--out` to keep variants side by side, without re-reading the sparse pixels or refitting. Each file records its provenance as HDF5 attributes (`provenance/`, `options`, `resolved`, `results`): the stage, a unique id, its source file and that file's id (checked when read, so a remade or replaced upstream file is refused), the anri version, git commit, date, working directory and command. The same stages are Python functions (`anri.index.stage_histogram`, `stage_search`, `stage_filter`, `stage_fit`, `stage_populations`, with `open_dataset`, `lit_map`, `select_by_likelihood`, `select_populations`, `show`, `read_provenance`, `upstream`), for notebooks and workflow systems; `anri.index.fit_occupancy` takes a `callback`. Replaces `<tag>.npz` (now the fit and populations files) and `<tag>_params.toml` (now each file's provenance); `tomli-w` is no longer a dependency. Results are bit-identical to the single command's.
- Measured frames against a map's forward model: `python -m anri.render prepare --dataset <dataset.h5>` (and `stage_prepare(dataset=...)`) simulates an ImageD11 dataset's own scan (every frame's omega and dty, with dty's motion within each row, the detector's shape, its y0 and geometry unless given). `anri.render.diagnostics.observed_row` (a row's sparse pixels, moved by the detector distortion to where an ideal detector sees them), `raw_image` (raw frames from the master file, likewise), `model_row`, `row_scale` (one least-squares factor per row, censored at the segmentation cut) and `frames_image`; `anri.render.plots.plot_frame_compare` (data, model and both overlaid, with `update(row, frame, frames)`). - `anri.fwd.render_row`'s stats give each peak's `in_range`, the share of its omega profile within the scan's omega range, and `captured` no longer counts the frames past the scan's ends (clipped copies of the end frame, which over-counted the capture of peaks near the ends; pixels unchanged). `captured / in_range` is the share of what the scan sees that the window holds: the rendering stats' `captured_min` uses it for the peaks centred in the scan (`in_range` >= 0.5), and `n_outside` counts the others. On the AM316L phantom every peak centred in the scan keeps more than 99.6% of it; the few that looked badly captured were peaks within 0.65 deg of the scan's ends.

The indexer's `_entries.npz` also holds the voxel size, lattice, space group and phase name.

- One rotation construction, in `anri.geom`: `skew(v)` (the cross-product matrix) and `rmat_from_axis_angle(axis, angle)`, now batched (Rodrigues' formula without a matrix product). `anri.crystal.rod_to_mat` and the renderer's orientation spread build on `skew`; `anri.phantom.axis_angle` is gone (use `anri.geom.rmat_from_axis_angle`). Faster: axis-angle 1.9x on CPU for 1e5 rotations (was `jax.scipy` `Rotation`), `rod_to_mat` 4-9x on CPU and 1.5-2.5x on GPU, the phantom's host rotations 5x; renders unchanged (bit-identical, same time).
- One source for orientation averages and angles in `anri.crystal`: `rotation_angle` (from the axial part and the trace, via atan2), `closest_equivalent` (a symmetric equivalent nearest a reference, and the angle) and `mean_rotation` (weighted SVD projection, always a proper rotation). `disorientation`, `to_fundamental_zone`, `anri.index.populations` and `anri.phantom.tensormap` use them. Population spreads lose a float32 noise floor: arccos of the trace quantised every spread below ~0.03 degrees (single-orientation populations reported 0.028 degrees; now ~1e-6). `phantom.tensormap`'s grain means no longer risk an improper matrix (a missing determinant fix), and its misorientations of float32 maps are accurate to ~1e-5 degrees instead of ~0.03.
- Lorentz and polarisation live in `anri.diffract`: `lorentz` and `polarisation` (moved from `anri.fwd`), and `lorentz_polarisation(k_in, k_out, wedge, chi, pol_factor)`, the per-reflection product the renderer and the indexer each computed themselves. `anri.index.predict` returns it with each prediction, (eta, omega, valid, lp), so the Ewald condition is solved once; `anri.index.lorentz_polarisation` is gone. Outputs bit-identical.
- `anri.geom.recon_to_sample`: reconstruction indices to sample positions (`recon_to_step`, then `step_to_sample`), used by `recon_positions` and `anri.io.entries_from_tensormap` (which called ImageD11's). `anri.index.fit_occupancy(coarse=...)` builds its coarse grid with `recon_positions` instead of its own meshgrid: the same positions, in ImageD11's order. Fine voxels midway between coarse voxels have no unique 9 nearest, so which equally near neighbours they inherit candidates from can change, and coarse runs differ slightly.
- `anri.crystal.U_B_to_UBI`: the one (U B)^-1, used by the indexer's predictions and results and by `anri.phantom.tensormap` (each inverted U @ B itself). The indexer's predictions now use the explicit 3x3 inverse (`anri.utils.inv3`); results agree to float32 rounding.
- `anri.geom.wrap_omega` (into [om0, om0 + 360)) and `nearest_omega` (the turn nearest a reference): the indexer's histogram binning, `dty_offsets` and the renderer each wrapped omega inline. `dty_offsets` now bins omega exactly as `anri.index.system` does, so its offsets always line up with the histogram's omega bins.
- `anri.geom.dty_to_row`: the one frame-to-row assignment, used by `anri.io.stream_sparse` and `anri.index.dty_offsets` (which had separate copies that had to agree).
- `anri.utils.fwhm_to_sigma` and `sigma_to_fwhm`: the one Gaussian FWHM conversion. `python -m anri.index` used both 2.355 and 2 sqrt(2 ln 2); it now uses the exact factor everywhere, so its default beam and bandwidth widths change by 0.008%.
- One forward-model centroid, one scattering origin. `anri.fwd.get_centroid` (and `get_centroid_both`) projects (ubi, hkl) to (sc, fc, omega) with the voxel at its real position for a given dty; the renderer uses it instead of its own copy, `get_centroid_box` is it at dty = y0 for a cube, and `get_centroid_scan` (which solves for the dty that centres the voxel on a pencil beam) shares its origin. `anri.geom.origin_lab` is where a voxel scatters (a column raised to a tilted beam's height), used by `get_centroid` and `dty_and_origin_lab`; `anri.geom.beam_offsets` is a point's position relative to the beam's centre line, used by `origin_lab` and `anri.fwd.beam_weight`. Outputs are unchanged: renders, peak selection, and scan and box centroids and covariances are bit-identical, except for tilted beams (differences ~1e-15 relative, from the order of operations).
- `python -m anri.index`: `--iter` defaults to 50 MLEM iterations (was 10). Features 1-2 voxels thin, such as twin lamellae, are still converging long after the deviance flattens: at 10 iterations a twin lost to its parent in voxels it fills. On a phantom, more iterations keep improving the map (pure voxels wrong: 0.36% at 10, 0.22% at 30, 0.14% at 100).
- `anri.index.histogram_pixels` takes a list of `((b_e, b_o, n_e, n_o), n_rows)` and fills all of them in one pass over the data. `python -m anri.index` reads the sparse pixels once again (it read them twice, which doubled the slowest step on large scans), and preparing each chunk is cheaper.
- Tests run on CPU by default, as in CI (`conftest.py` sets `JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu` unless it is set): on a GPU they mostly waited for XLA:GPU to compile small one-off shapes.
- `anri.fwd.render_peaks` weights each frame by the beam at that frame's own omega (the mean omega of the peak's mass within the frame), not at the peak's centroid omega. A peak spread in omega (e.g. by `"sig_rot"`) diffracts at different omegas, where a voxel off the rotation axis sits at a different place across the beam, so its intensity spreads over dty rows along the voxel's sinusoid. For a 0.5 degree spread 50 um off the axis with a 0.25 um beam, the intensity per row was off by ~150% and is now within ~6% of explicitly rotated sub-entries. `select_peaks` widens its reach across the beam by the voxel's motion within the omega margin. Sharp peaks are essentially unchanged.
- `anri.fwd.render_peaks` centres each frame's pixel window on the peak's mean position in that frame (it used to centre every frame on the peak's overall centroid), so a peak that moves across the detector with omega stays inside its window. Peaks within one or two frames are unchanged.
- `anri.fwd.render_row` is faster on GPUs: duplicate pixels (neighbouring voxels light up the same pixels, ~100x for a grain) are summed on the device, so only unique pixels go to the host, and batches are 1024 peaks per device times a power of 4, so a scan compiles at most a few shapes (XLA:GPU took up to a minute to compile very small batches). A 153-row scan of a 6818-voxel grain renders in 1 minute instead of 3. Output is unchanged up to float rounding; CPUs keep the host merge, as XLA's CPU sort is slower than NumPy's.
- For a beam tilted out of the horizontal plane, the scattering origin in the scanning model is where the pencil crosses the voxel's column: the beam is taken to cross the rotation axis at the voxel's own height (the layer's height in a 2D map), so the origin is raised by (k_z / k_x) x. Nothing changes for a horizontal beam.
- The renderer handles pencil, line and box beams (e.g. DCT) with one model. Every voxel sits at its real lab position for the row's dty (it used to be moved onto the pencil's centre line), and `anri.fwd.beam_weight` (replacing `dty_weight`) integrates the beam's profile across it over the voxel: horizontally and vertically, each a flat top blurred by a Gaussian (`geom_from_pars(..., sig_beam, width_beam, sig_beam_v, width_beam_v)`). Voxels are columns for 2D maps or cubes for 3D maps (`voxel_3d`). The beam can point anywhere (`k_in_lab`): it sees the voxel rotated by omega - psi, and travels 1/cos(alpha) further through a column. `select_peaks` keeps peaks whose voxel is within reach of the beam.
- `anri.fwd.polarisation(k_in, k_out, factor)` takes the beam direction: horizontal polarisation is across the beam, wherever it points.

### Removed

- The crystallography classes `anri.crystal.UnitCell`, `Symmetry`, `Crystal`, `Structure` and `Grain`. Use the functions above: e.g. `B_matrix(lpars)` for `Crystal.B`, `symmetry_matrices(sg)` for `Symmetry.sym_matrices`, `reflections` + `structure_factors` + `rings` for `Structure.make_hkls` and `rings_table`. Reflections are no longer filtered by |F|²: keep `structure_factors(...) > 0.01` for the old set.

### Fixed

- `anri.index.stages.run_command` on a chain with a fit but no local search gives `--levels 0`: `run` would otherwise add a local search the files never had.
- `anri.maths.rotation.rmat_from_axis_angle` with an integer axis (e.g. `[0, 0, 1]`) no longer truncates the angle to whole degrees: an integer axis is taken as floating point ([#34](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/34)).
- `python -m anri.simulate run`: the positional output dataset name and `--dataset` (an ImageD11 DataSet to simulate) shared one argparse destination, so without `--dataset` the run tried to open the output name as an input DataSet, and with it the output was named after the input path. They are now separate ([#25](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/25)).
- A stage given an output file that is one of its inputs (`--out` naming its source, a file further up the chain, or the DataSet, map or mask it reads) erased it: the file was opened for writing before anything else. Every stage of `anri.index` and `anri.simulate` now refuses such an output first (`anri.io.provenance.check_out`, also through links), and the input is kept ([#26](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/26)).
- `anri.index.occupancy.fit_occupancy` failed in `top_k` when fewer orientations were kept than the candidates asked for (`--cand`, default 64): it now takes as many candidates as there are orientations, and refuses an empty list with a clear message ([#32](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/32)).
- `anri.geom.scan.step_grid_from_ybincens` with `gridstep > 1` could leave 0 off the grid (e.g. -17, -15, ..., 17), so the grid was no longer centred where `step_to_recon` puts the rotation axis. The half extent is now rounded up to whole grid steps, as ImageD11 does after its release 2.1.3 ([#33](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/33)).
- `anri.fwd.render` compiled a new slice for every count of rendered pixels it copied back from a device, which changes from batch to batch. The slice is now rounded up to a power of 2 (at most twice the pixels needed) and cut on the host, so a scan compiles a few lengths instead of one per batch ([#35](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/35)).
- `anri.index.data.detector_coverage` counted only the part of a ring's window on the detector, so a (ring, eta bin) running off the detector's edge read as fully covered, but as part covered if masked pixels extended the detector. It is now also scaled by the share of the bin's window (2theta within the tolerance, eta within the bin) that reaches the detector. Histogram files made before keep their coverage: remake them to use it ([#30](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/30)).
- `anri.index.populations.group` left out candidates below 2% of a voxel's total, so a voxel whose occupancy was spread thinly over many orientations had no population, yet was exported with the identity orientation. The cut `eps` is now relative to the voxel's most occupied candidate: such a voxel comes out as one population with a large spread ([#27](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/27)).
- `anri.index.populations.group` returned populations in the order they were seeded, not largest first as documented, so the main population (column 0, used by the TensorMap) could be the smaller one. They are now sorted by fraction ([#29](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/29)).
- `python -m anri.index populations` exported each population's rms misorientation as `sig_rot`, which the renderer reads as the standard deviation of each component of the rotation: renders from `_entries.npz` were sqrt(3) too wide. It now exports the spread / sqrt(3) ([#31](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/31)).
- `python -m anri.index --coarse`: a fine voxel's candidates from its coarse neighbourhood (`anri.index.occupancy.inherit_candidates`) ranked an orientation found in several neighbours by whichever copy sorted first, so a strong one could be dropped for a weak copy, and repeated an orientation when the neighbourhood held fewer than 2k. An orientation now ranks by its largest occupancy in any neighbour, comes once, and empty slots are -1, which `candidates_from` leaves out ([#28](https://github.com/jadball/anri/issues/28)).
- `python -m anri.index filter` (`anri.index.orientations.fit`) kept a different set of orientations each run on a GPU. The likelihood ratio summed `d log(mu / (mu - g a))` in float32: for a weak orientation, mu / (mu - g a) rounds to 1 plus a few ulps, and times ~1e6 counts per row-summed bin that is noise of order 1 in the ratio. The GPU's summation order changes the occupancies by ~1e-6 from run to run, and the noise turned that into 0.1-5 in the ratio: on AP1_1, 725 orientations crossed `--min-lr 1` between two runs on the same input. It now uses `log1p`: with plain MLEM, two runs then kept the same orientations at 1, 25 and 100 (with SQUAREM, see Changed, 20-50 of 24,000 still differ). The maps hardly depend on it (orientations below a ratio of 100 held no occupancy there).
- `anri.index.system`, `backproject` and `reconstruct` put a voxel in the dty rows with a beam along lab x and an untilted rotation axis, whatever the geometry: they ignored wedge, chi and the beam's direction. They now use `anri.geom.find_dty_for_beam_xy` (now broadcasting over points and omegas, elementwise, so it is cheap on the indexer's hot path), with optional scan keys "k_in_lab", "wedge" and "chi" (default: untilted, along x); `python -m anri.index` passes its geometry's. With a beam profile the voxel's chord is taken at omega - psi, as in `anri.fwd.beam_weight`. Untilted geometries are unchanged.
- `anri.index.fit_occupancy(coarse=...)` (`python -m anri.index --coarse`) on fly and helical scans: the coarse pass kept the fine rows' `ddty` and `exposure`, so coarse row k used fine row k's dty offsets. The new `anri.index.coarsen_scan` gives the coarse scan of rows summed in groups (also used for `--gridstep`), with each group's mean exposure and mean real dty. Step scans are unchanged.
- `anri.fwd` (`bin_fractions`, `truncated_moments`, so every rendered pixel): a Gaussian's mass in a bin far in its upper tail, Phi(b) - Phi(a), cancelled in float32 to about 1e-7 of either sign (2% off at 5 sigma, 0 instead of 5e-10 at 6 sigma). Times a bright peak's amplitude, and summed over the many voxels sharing a pixel, it made pixels far from peaks wrong, even negative (a log-likelihood of them was NaN). The upper tail now uses Phi(-a) - Phi(-b), and a mass is never negative.
- `python -m anri.index`: the measured / fitted intensity per dty row (`row_ratio`) now sums only the eta bins the fit models. It included the data at |sin eta| <= `--etacut`, which the fit does not predict, so every row read too high.
- `anri.io.write_pars` writes `t_x`, `t_y`, `t_z` (0) and `omegasign` (1) when the geometry lacks them: ImageD11 needs them to compute peak geometry (`DataSet.update_colfile_pars` raised a KeyError on simulated datasets).
- `anri.index.inherit_candidates` finds each voxel's coarse neighbours with a KD-tree: sorting every distance took ~2 minutes on a 419 × 419 map, more than the candidate pass that `--coarse` saves (now ~4 s).
- `python -m anri.index` writes IPF (`ipf_x/y/z`) and Euler maps into its TensorMap again, and a ParaView `.xdmf` beside it, as the sandbox script did. Strain maps are left out: these UBIs are rotations of the nominal lattice, so the strain would be exactly zero.
- `anri.fwd.render_row` with `"sig_rot"` failed under newer JAX (shard_map's check that stacked arrays vary across the same devices): the rotation's cross-product matrix is now a sum over constant generators.
- Wrong spot positions in float32 on recent NVIDIA GPUs: JAX's default precision for float32 matrix products there is TF32 (10-bit mantissa), which moved rendered spots ~1000 px from the beam centre by up to 0.3 px on an L40S, a strain error of ~1e-5. `anri.utils.setup()` now sets `jax_default_matmul_precision` to `"highest"` (unless `JAX_DEFAULT_MATMUL_PRECISION` is set). GPU renders made in float32 without this fix should be redone.
- Beam divergence (`ky`, `kz`) was applied along lab y and z, which is only across the beam when it is along lab x: for a tilted beam the vertical divergence shrank by cos(tilt). It is now applied along the beam's own horizontal and vertical (`anri.geom.beam_basis`), in every forward model.
- `anri.geom.step_grid_from_ybincens` always raised: it was jitted, but the size of its grid depends on its inputs.
- `render_peaks` put up to ~3% of a peak's intensity in the wrong cells (and lost up to ~7% from broad, truncated peaks): when conditioning fast on slow it held omega at its frame mean, ignoring that slow and omega are correlated within the frame. It now integrates omega out within the frame. Errors against a Monte Carlo of the beam spreads went from 1% (median worst cell) to 0.13%, the Monte Carlo noise.
- Wrong renders on CPU with jaxlib >= 0.11: XLA:CPU's YNNPACK fusions miscompiled `render_peaks` for batches of more than a few thousand peaks (in float64, most peaks squeezed into one pixel; in float32, NaNs). `anri.utils.setup()` now turns them off with `--xla_cpu_experimental_ynn_fusion_type=`. CPU renders made with jaxlib 0.11.x without this fix should be redone.
- `anri.crystal.__all__` and `anri.fwd.__all__` listed functions that don't exist, so `from anri.crystal import *` failed. `anri.fwd` now exports `propagate_cov_scan_both`.
